Reaching 70 is a milestone, and in Cyprus it also means your driving licence renewal works differently from now on. The rules are not designed to push older drivers off the road. They are designed to check that you are still safe behind the wheel. Knowing exactly what is required makes the whole process much less stressful.

## Why does the renewal process change for drivers over 70?

Cyprus follows European Union rules on driving licences. The key law is EU Directive 2006/126/EC, which Cyprus has written into national law. That directive allows member states to require more frequent renewals and medical checks for drivers once they pass a certain age threshold.

The thinking behind this is straightforward. As people age, certain health conditions can affect driving ability. Vision changes, reaction times, and physical mobility can all matter when you are in control of a vehicle. Regular renewals give both the driver and the authorities a chance to check that nothing has changed in a way that could be dangerous.

For drivers under 70, a standard Category B licence can be valid for a much longer period. Once you turn 70, that changes. Your licence will be issued for a shorter validity period, and each renewal requires a medical certificate. This is not a punishment. It is a periodic health check with a specific purpose.

## What documents and medical certificates do you need?

Getting your paperwork in order before you visit a transport office saves time and avoids a wasted trip. The documents you will typically need include the following:

- Your current Cyprus driving licence (or the licence you are renewing).
- A valid identity document - your Cyprus ID card or passport.
- A completed medical certificate of fitness to drive, issued by an approved practitioner.
- A recent passport-style photograph.
- The applicable renewal fee (check current fees with the Department of Road Transport).

The medical certificate is the part that trips people up most often. Not every doctor can issue it. The certificate must come from a practitioner who is recognised for this purpose by the Department of Road Transport. Before you book a doctor's appointment, ask the ΤΟΜ for their current approved list. That list can change, and using a doctor who is not on it means your certificate will not be accepted.

If you hold licences in more than one category - say Category B for a standard car and BE for towing a trailer - the medical certificate and the renewal application must cover each relevant category. Do not assume that renewing one category automatically covers the others.

## Step by step: how to submit your renewal application at the Department of Road Transport

The process is not complicated, but knowing the order of steps helps. Here is how it typically works:

1. **Confirm your eligibility and timing.** Check the expiry date on your current licence. Do not wait until it has already expired. A lapsed licence means you cannot legally drive until the renewal is done.
2. **Get your medical certificate.** Book an appointment with a doctor on the approved list. Allow enough time before your licence expires for the appointment and any follow-up.
3. **Gather all required documents.** See the checklist in the section above.
4. **Visit your local District Transport Office.** Offices are in Nicosia, Limassol, Larnaca, and Paphos. Choose the one closest to you or the one that covers your district.
5. **Submit your application and pay the fee.** Hand over your documents at the counter. Staff will check everything and take your payment. Confirm the current fee before you go.
6. **Wait for your new licence.** Processing times vary. Ask the office how long to expect and whether any temporary permit is issued in the meantime.

## How often must you renew your licence after the age of 70?

This is one of the most common questions people ask, and the honest answer is: it depends on what your medical assessment shows and what the Department of Road Transport determines for your specific case.

What is clear is that once you are over 70, the validity period for your licence is shorter than it would be for a younger driver. Cyprus law, following EU Directive 2006/126/EC, allows for shorter renewal cycles for older drivers. The exact period printed on your new licence is what applies to you.

Some drivers find that after a straightforward renewal with no medical concerns, they receive a licence valid for a standard shorter period. Others, where a health condition has been noted, may receive a licence with an even shorter validity. In that case, you would need to renew again sooner and provide an updated medical certificate at that point.

The key practical rule is simple: do not wait for a reminder. Cyprus does not guarantee that a renewal notice will arrive in your post. Check the expiry date on your licence, note it somewhere obvious, and start the renewal process well in advance. Starting at least two to three months before expiry gives you enough time to get the medical certificate and deal with any delays at the transport office.

Driving on an expired licence in Cyprus is not permitted. If your licence expires and you have not yet completed the renewal, you must stop driving on public roads until the new licence is in your hands.

## What does the medical assessment involve and what should you expect?

The medical assessment is a check of your general fitness to drive. It is not an intensive examination, but it is thorough enough to identify conditions that could affect road safety.

The doctor carrying out the assessment will typically look at several areas. Vision is one of the most important. You need to be able to see clearly enough to read road signs, judge distances, and react to hazards. If you wear glasses or contact lenses to drive, bring them to the appointment. Your licence may end up with a condition requiring you to wear them while driving.

Physical fitness matters too. The doctor may check your movement, particularly whether you can turn your head to check blind spots, use pedals, and handle the steering wheel without difficulty. If you have a condition affecting your limbs or coordination, mention it. There are licence conditions and vehicle adaptations that can allow people to keep driving safely with certain physical challenges.

Heart conditions, diabetes, epilepsy, and neurological conditions are all areas a doctor will ask about. You do not need a perfect bill of health to pass. You need to show that any conditions you have are managed and do not pose an unacceptable risk while driving.

Be honest with the doctor. Withholding information about a health condition that later causes an accident creates serious legal problems. The assessment is there to help you stay on the road safely, not to look for reasons to stop you driving.

After the assessment, the doctor will complete the official medical certificate. If the outcome is clear, you take that certificate and use it in your renewal application. If the doctor has concerns, they may refer you for further specialist tests before completing the certificate.

## Can a licence be issued with restrictions or special conditions?

Yes. The Department of Road Transport has the authority to issue a driving licence with specific conditions or restrictions noted on the document. This is more common for older drivers, but it can apply at any age.

Restrictions are not automatically a bad thing. In many cases, they allow a driver to keep their licence when they might otherwise lose it. A condition that says you must drive only during daylight hours, for example, might reflect a vision issue that does not affect you in good light. That is a practical compromise that keeps you mobile.

Common types of conditions that may appear on a Cyprus licence include:

- A requirement to wear corrective lenses while driving.
- A restriction to certain types of vehicle, such as vehicles with automatic transmission.
- A geographic restriction limiting driving to local areas.
- A requirement for vehicle adaptations, such as hand controls for drivers with limited leg mobility.
- A shorter validity period requiring more frequent renewal.

If a medical condition is identified during the renewal process, the ΤΟΜ will decide what conditions, if any, to attach. You will be informed of any restrictions before the licence is issued. If you disagree with a decision, you can ask the department to explain it and, in some cases, seek a review.

It is worth knowing that driving with a condition you do not meet - for example, driving without glasses when your licence says you must wear them - is a legal offence in Cyprus. Make sure you understand every condition on your licence and follow it every time you drive.

## FAQ

**Does my Cyprus driving licence expire automatically at age 70?**
No, it does not expire automatically when you turn 70. Your licence expires on the date printed on it. However, once you are over 70, each renewal will cover a shorter period than a standard licence, and you will need to provide a medical certificate with every renewal application.

**Can I renew my licence by post or online, or must I attend in person?**
As of current practice, renewal applications for drivers over 70 in Cyprus are submitted in person at a District Transport Office in Nicosia, Limassol, Larnaca, or Paphos. Contact the Department of Road Transport to confirm whether any online or postal options have been introduced since your last renewal.

**What happens if my doctor says I am not fit to drive?**
If the approved medical practitioner determines you are not fit to drive, they will not issue a medical certificate. Without that certificate, the Department of Road Transport cannot process your renewal. You have the option to seek a second opinion or to discuss the situation further with the department, but you must not drive while your licence is expired or revoked.

**How much does it cost to renew a driving licence over 70 in Cyprus?**
Fees are set by the Department of Road Transport and can change over time. Check current fees with the Department of Road Transport before you apply. Do not rely on figures quoted by other drivers or older online sources, as these may be out of date.

**Do the same medical rules apply if I only drive occasionally or short distances?**
Yes. The medical certificate requirement and the shorter renewal period apply regardless of how often or how far you drive. There is no exemption for low-mileage drivers. If your licence is in a category that requires renewal with a medical certificate, that applies to every driver in that category over the age of 70.

## How do driving school prices work in Limassol?

Most schools in Limassol sell lessons in one of three ways. You can pay for each lesson as you go. You can buy a package of lessons for one price. Or you can ask for a quote for the whole course, from theory to test day.

Each way suits a different learner. Paying per lesson gives you freedom. You can pause for a holiday, change school, or stop without losing money. The cost is that each lesson may be priced higher, and you have no fixed total to plan around.

A package usually lowers the price of each lesson. It also ties you to one school. If you pass early, you may have lessons left over. If you need more lessons than the package holds, the extra ones may be charged at a different rate. Ask what happens in both cases before you pay anything.

A full-course quote is the easiest to budget. It can also hide the most. One number may cover theory, lessons, test preparation and a car for test day. Another school's number may cover only some of those items. Without a line-by-line list, you cannot tell which one is cheaper.

## What changes the price of a lesson or a package?

Two schools on the same street can quote very different prices, and there are often good reasons. These are the main factors to look for when you read a quote:

- Lesson length. A lesson may be a full hour or shorter. Check how many minutes you actually drive.
- Car type. A manual car and an automatic car may be priced differently.
- Package size. A bigger package may bring a lower price per lesson.
- Theory course. It may be included in the price, sold separately, or not offered at all.
- Test-day car. Some schools charge for the car and the instructor on test day. Others include them.
- Pick-up. A school may collect you from home or work, or ask you to meet at its office.
- Extras. These can include registration paperwork and the first-aid course.

Pick-up is another small item that adds up. A school that collects you from your door may build that service into the price. A school that asks you to meet at its office may quote less, but you spend your own travel time. Neither choice is wrong. Just make sure the quote says which one applies.

Lesson length matters more than most people think. A lesson that is shorter than you expected hides a higher hourly price. Ask for the length in minutes. Ask whether travel time to your meeting point counts inside it.

Car type is worth a question at the start. If you plan to learn in an automatic, tell the school early. Ask which car you will use on test day. Then check with the Department of Road Transport (ΤΟΜ) how the type of car affects the licence you receive.

Test-day costs are easy to miss. The test fee itself is set by the Department of Road Transport, so check current fees with ΤΟΜ. The car and the instructor's time on the day may be charged separately by the school. If a quote does not mention test day, ask about it before you sign.

## How can you compare quotes fairly?

A fair comparison needs the same items on every quote. Write each quote down in the same layout. Then you can see what really differs between schools.

Use this checklist for every school you contact:

1. Price per lesson, and the length of each lesson in minutes.
2. Number of lessons in the package.
3. Price of extra lessons after the package ends.
4. Theory course: included, extra, or not offered.
5. First-aid course: included or extra.
6. Test-day car and instructor: included or extra.
7. Registration or administration fees charged by the school.
8. Refund rules for lessons you do not use.
9. Payment terms: everything upfront, or in instalments.

Once you have the numbers, work out a total for each school. Multiply the lessons by the price, then add every extra item. The calculator below lets you try different lesson counts. Use it with the prices from your own quotes.

## What should you ask a Limassol driving school before you pay?

Questions show you how a school works. A good school answers clearly and puts the answers in writing. A school that avoids your questions tells you something too.

Bring this list when you call or visit:

- Is the school, and each instructor, licensed by the Department of Road Transport (ΤΟΜ)?
- Can I have this quote in writing, with every item listed?
- Which language will my lessons be in?
- Can I take one trial lesson before I buy a package?
- Which car will I use, and will it be the same car on test day?
- How do I book lessons, and what is the cancellation rule?
- What happens to unused lessons if I pass early or move away?

The trial lesson is the most useful step. Ask whether you can pay for a single lesson first. Treat it like an interview of the school. Notice how the instructor explains things and how they react when you make a mistake.

## What does the licence process look like for a learner in Limassol?

School fees are only part of the picture. The Department of Road Transport (ΤΟΜ) handles the official steps, and it serves Limassol through its district office there. Check the current booking rules and opening hours on the department's official website before you travel.

The usual steps for a car licence look like this:

1. Get a medical certificate from a registered doctor.
2. Apply for your learner permit (Άδεια Μαθητευόμενου) through ΤΟΜ.
3. Prepare for the theory test and sit it.
4. Complete the first-aid course, if it is required for your case.
5. Take your practical lessons with a licensed instructor.
6. Sit the practical test.
7. Collect your licence once everything is approved.

The order and the rules can change, so confirm them with ΤΟΜ. Ask your school whether it helps with forms and bookings or leaves them to you. That help can save you time, and it should appear on the quote.

Government fees are separate from what the school charges. Our guide to [Cyprus driving licence fees](/en/blog/cyprus-driving-licence-fees) shows which costs go to the state. It also shows which go to doctors, schools and course providers. Always check current amounts with ΤΟΜ.

## How can you keep costs under control without cutting corners?

Good value is not the same as the lowest price. The goal is to pay for what you need and nothing you do not. These habits help:

- Book one trial lesson before you buy a package.
- Ask whether you can start with a smaller block and top up later.
- Study the theory before your practical lessons begin.
- Book lessons close together, so you do not forget what you learned.
- Keep the written quote and every receipt in one folder.
- Compare the total for the whole course, not only the price per lesson.
- Ask about payment in instalments if paying upfront is hard.

Be careful with an offer that sits far below every other quote. Ask what is missing from it. It may leave out the theory course, the test-day car or the first-aid course. A low price that grows later is not a low price.

Regular practice outside lessons also helps, where the law allows it. Ask your instructor what practice is legal and useful for your stage. Fewer wasted lessons usually means a lower total bill.

Finally, remember that the fees paid to the Department of Road Transport do not change from school to school. What differs is the school's own price and what it includes. Spend your comparison time there.

Choosing a driving school in Limassol comes down to clear quotes and honest answers. Ask for everything in writing, compare the same items, and book a trial lesson before you commit. Check government fees with ΤΟΜ rather than relying on a school's summary. A little time spent now can save you money and stress later, and it helps you pass with confidence.

## FAQ

**How much does a driving lesson cost in Limassol?**
It depends on the school, the length of the lesson and the type of car. We do not list prices here because schools set them and change them. Ask three or four schools for a written quote and compare the same items. Check test and licence fees with the Department of Road Transport.

**Is a package cheaper than paying per lesson in Limassol?**
A package often lowers the price of each lesson. It only saves money if you use every lesson you buy. Ask what happens to unused lessons and what extra lessons cost. A trial lesson first can help you decide.

**Can I take driving lessons in English in Limassol?**
Many schools in Cyprus teach in Greek and English, and some offer other languages. Limassol has a large international community, so English lessons are often available. Ask each school which languages its instructors speak before you book.

**Does the school price include the test fee?**
Not always. The Department of Road Transport sets the test fee, and schools may list it separately or leave it out. Ask whether the quote covers the test fee, the test-day car and the instructor. Then confirm current fees with ΤΟΜ.

**How many lessons will I need before the practical test?**
Cyprus has no fixed legal minimum number of lessons for a Category B car licence. The number depends on your skill, your past experience and how often you practise. Most schools can give you an honest estimate after a trial lesson.

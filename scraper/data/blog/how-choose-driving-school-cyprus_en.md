Most people spend more time picking a phone plan than picking a driving school. That is a mistake. The school you choose affects how long it takes to pass, how much it costs you, and how confident you feel on Cyprus roads long after the test is done. Getting this decision right from the start saves you time, money, and stress.

## Why Does Choosing the Right Driving School in Cyprus Matter?

Cyprus roads have their own character. Traffic can be heavy in cities, roundabouts appear often, and the driving culture is different from much of mainland Europe. Add to that the fact that Cyprus drives on the left - a legacy of British rule - and you have real adjustment challenges if you learned to drive elsewhere.

A good school prepares you for all of this. A poor one just gets you through enough lessons to sit the test. The gap between those two outcomes is wider than most new learners expect.

Every driving school in Cyprus must be licensed by the Department of Road Transport, known locally as ΤΟΜ. That licence is the minimum standard. It tells you the school is legal. It does not tell you whether the instructors are patient, whether the cars are modern, or whether the school will still answer your calls once you have paid. Those things require a little more digging.

The stakes are also financial. Lessons are not cheap. If you end up at a school that teaches badly, you may need more lessons before you are ready to test. You might fail the practical test and pay again to retake it. Choosing carefully at the start is far cheaper than fixing a bad choice later.

For newcomers especially, language is another factor. Many schools in Cyprus offer lessons in both Greek and English. Some offer Russian or Arabic too. Finding a school that teaches in your strongest language means you absorb the rules faster and feel more at ease during lessons.

Read the [step-by-step driving licence guide](/en/blog/how-to-get-driving-licence-cyprus-step-by-step) to understand the full process before you even call a school.

## Pass Rates: How to Evaluate Them?

Pass rates are the single most talked-about number in driving school comparisons. They matter, but you need to read them carefully.

Here is the honest situation in Cyprus: ΤΟΜ does not publish school-by-school pass rate data in a standard public format. That means you cannot look up a league table online. You have to ask each school directly.

When a school quotes you a pass rate, ask these questions:

- Is that the first-attempt pass rate or the overall rate including retakes?
- How many students does that figure cover and over what time period?
- Does it include all students or only those who completed the full package?

A school that has coached 200 students in a year is giving you more reliable data than one quoting a figure from 15 students last spring. Small samples can look impressive by luck.

Also think about what a high pass rate can mean in practice. Some schools push students to test before they are truly ready, simply to chase numbers. Others are selective about who they let sit the test, which inflates the figure. Neither approach necessarily means you will be a good driver at the end.

The pass rate is a useful starting point. It is not the whole story. Use it as one signal alongside instructor quality and lesson structure.

## How to Judge Instructor Quality?

An instructor licence issued by the Department of Road Transport is the legal baseline. Every instructor in Cyprus must hold one, and they must renew it periodically. Ask to see evidence of this. A reputable school will not mind the question at all.

Beyond the licence, you are looking for qualities that no certificate measures directly. Is the instructor calm under pressure? Do they explain why, not just what? Do they adjust their teaching style when you are struggling with something? These things become obvious very quickly in a trial lesson.

Most schools will offer a first lesson at a standard rate before you commit to a package. Take it. Treat it as an interview of the school, not just a lesson. Pay attention to how the instructor handles a mistake. Criticism that makes you tense is counterproductive. Good correction builds confidence.

## Lesson Costs: What Does the Price Actually Include?

Price varies between schools, between cities, and depending on whether you pay per lesson or buy a package. Before you compare numbers, make sure you are comparing the same thing.

A quoted price per lesson tells you very little on its own. You need to know:

- Does the price include theory lessons or only practical driving?
- Is the first-aid course included? (This is a mandatory part of the licence process in Cyprus - confirm current requirements with the Department of Road Transport.)
- Are test preparation sessions included?
- What happens if you fail the test? Do you pay again for extra lessons?

Package deals are common in Cyprus. A bundle covering theory, practical lessons, and test prep can work out cheaper than paying per session. But only if the number of lessons in the package matches what you actually need. There is no legally fixed minimum number of lessons for a Category B licence in Cyprus. Some people are ready in fewer lessons. Others need more. A package that caps your lessons may cost you extra anyway.

For a detailed breakdown of what to expect to pay, read about [driving lesson costs in Cyprus](/en/blog/driving-lesson-cost-cyprus).

## Location and Schedule Flexibility: Why Do They Matter?

A school that is far from where you live or work is a school you will eventually resent. Lessons require consistency. If getting there is a hassle, you will reschedule more often, and your progress will slow.

Cyprus has four main cities where driving schools are concentrated: Nicosia, Limassol, Larnaca, and Paphos. Most learners can find a reasonable number of options within their own city. If you are in a smaller town or village, your choices narrow, and travelling to the nearest city may be unavoidable.

For learners based in the capital, looking at [driving schools in Nicosia](/en/driving-schools/nicosia) by area within the city can still make a difference. Nicosia traffic in some districts is significantly heavier than in others, and a school based in your neighbourhood saves you time every week.

Ask about lesson timing before you commit. Some schools only offer lessons during standard working hours. If you work full-time, that is a real problem. Evening and weekend slots fill up fast. Schools with a large pool of instructors can usually offer more flexibility.

Also ask whether the route of practical lessons includes the roads near the test centre. Familiarity with the actual roads used during the test helps. Some schools are deliberate about this. Others just drive wherever is convenient on the day.

## Training Vehicles and Facilities: What to Check?

The car you learn in matters. Vehicles used for Cyprus driving lessons and tests must meet roadworthiness standards set by the Department of Road Transport. That is the legal floor. What you want, in practice, is something above that floor.

Newer dual-control cars are safer for training. The dual-control setup means the instructor has a brake pedal on their side. It gives you more confidence to try things without fear of a serious error. It also gives the instructor genuine control if something goes wrong.

Ask the school how old their vehicles are. Ask when the cars were last serviced. A well-maintained car signals that the school runs a professional operation. A tatty, high-mileage car with a cracked dashboard is not necessarily dangerous, but it suggests the school is not investing in its students.

For the theory side, check whether the school has a proper classroom or uses a dedicated online system. Theory in Cyprus covers road signs, rules, and traffic law. Good schools use structured materials, practice tests, and feedback. Some use software platforms that track your progress and flag weak areas. That is far more useful than reading a booklet alone.

## Practical Questions to Ask Before You Enrol

Before you sign anything, sit down with someone at the school and ask direct questions. Their answers - and the way they answer - will tell you a lot.

Here is a checklist of questions worth asking:

- Is the school fully licensed by the Department of Road Transport (ΤΟΜ)?
- Can I see the instructor's licence before we start?
- What is the school's reported pass rate, and how is it calculated?
- What exactly is included in the package price?
- Is the first-aid course included or extra?
- What language will my lessons be in?
- How do you match students with instructors?
- What happens if I need more lessons than the package covers?
- How soon can I book my first lesson?
- Do your practical lesson routes include roads near the test centre?

A school that answers these clearly and without irritation is one that has probably heard them before - because confident, quality schools get asked these things all the time. A school that deflects or rushes you is giving you useful information too.

Also check the [Cyprus driving licence categories](/en/blog/cyprus-driving-licence-categories) page before you enrol. The minimum age for a Category B car licence in Cyprus is 17 for supervised driving and 18 for a full licence. Make sure you are applying for the right category for your needs, whether that is a car, a motorbike, or something else.

Finally, read the [Cyprus driving test guide](/en/blog/cyprus-driving-test-2026) so you understand what the practical test actually involves. Knowing what the examiner looks for helps you ask the right questions of any school you visit.

Choosing a school is not glamorous. But the hour you spend researching now is worth far more than the money and time you might lose from a bad choice later.

## FAQ

**Does a driving school in Cyprus need to be officially registered?**
Yes. All driving schools in Cyprus must hold a valid licence from the Department of Road Transport (ΤΟΜ). Operating without one is illegal. Always confirm a school's licence status before you pay anything or sign a contract.

**How many lessons will I need before I can take the practical test in Cyprus?**
There is no legally fixed minimum number of lessons for a Category B car licence in Cyprus. The number depends on your ability, your prior experience, and how often you practise. Most schools will give you an honest estimate after your first lesson.

**Can I take driving lessons in English in Cyprus?**
Yes. Most driving schools in Cyprus offer lessons in both Greek and English. Some also offer instruction in Russian or Arabic to serve expat communities. Ask the school directly which languages their instructors teach in before you book.

**Is the first-aid course compulsory as part of getting a Cyprus driving licence?**
The standard learner process in Cyprus includes a mandatory first-aid course before the practical test. Requirements can change, so confirm the current rules with the Department of Road Transport (ΤΟΜ) and ask whether your chosen school includes the course in its package price.

**How do I compare the cost of different driving schools fairly?**
Make sure every quote you receive covers the same items: theory lessons, practical lessons, first-aid course, and test preparation. Ask whether the test fee itself is included or separate. Package deals can offer good value, but only if the number of lessons matches your likely needs. Check current test fees directly with ΤΟΜ, as they may change.

Moving to a new country always comes with a list of admin tasks. Swapping your driving licence is one most people put off. If you have settled in Cyprus and you hold a driving licence from another EU country, you are probably wondering whether you actually have to do anything at all. The short answer is: not always. But the longer answer is more useful.

## Can you drive in Cyprus on an EU licence?

Yes, you can. EU and EEA licence holders who are living in Cyprus are allowed to drive here on their home-country licence. Cyprus is an EU member state, and the mutual recognition of driving licences across EU countries applies here just as it does elsewhere in Europe.

This means you do not need to sit a new theory test. You do not need to take a driving test. You do not need to do anything immediately just to get behind the wheel legally.

That said, the rules around how long you can use your foreign licence without exchanging it are tied to your residency status. Once you are officially resident in Cyprus, a window period begins. During that period, you can apply to exchange your licence without any tests. The length of that window can change, so confirm the current deadline directly with the Department of Road Transport. Do not rely on what a friend told you two years ago.

If you are still in the early stages of your move and want a broader picture of what driving admin looks like here, the [licence guide for foreigners](/en/blog/how-to-get-driving-licence-cyprus-foreigner) is a helpful starting point. It covers different licence types and different situations in one place.

One more thing worth flagging: if your licence comes from the UK, the rules are different because of Brexit. That situation is covered separately in the guide on [UK licences in Cyprus after Brexit](/en/blog/uk-licence-cyprus-after-brexit).

## Why do most EU residents choose to exchange their licence?

Legally you may not have to exchange, but in practice most long-term residents do. The reasons are not dramatic. They are just practical.

The first is car rental. If you rent a car in Cyprus regularly, a Cypriot licence makes things smoother at the counter. Rental staff here are used to Cypriot licences. A German or Romanian licence can sometimes cause delays or extra questions, even if it is perfectly valid.

The second is local admin. Cyprus has a lot of paperwork. When you are dealing with banks, insurance companies, or government offices, a Cypriot document is always easier. People recognise it. They know what to look for. A foreign-language licence can slow things down.

The third is replacement. If your licence is lost or stolen, getting a replacement from your home country while you are living in Cyprus is a headache. You would need to contact the authority in another country, wait, and possibly travel. If you hold a Cypriot licence, you go to the local Department of Road Transport and sort it out there.

The fourth reason is that a Cypriot licence is valid across all EU member states. So you do not lose anything by exchanging. You can still drive in France, Italy, Greece, or anywhere else in the EU just as you could before.

For most people who have settled here permanently, exchanging just makes life easier. It is one less thing to worry about.

## What is the exchange process step by step?

The process is simpler than most people expect. There are no tests involved. You go in, submit your documents, and wait for your new licence to be issued. Here is how it works in practice.

1. Gather your documents before you go. The list is short but each item is required.
2. Visit a Department of Road Transport office. There are offices in Nicosia, Limassol, Larnaca, Paphos, and Paralimni.
3. Submit your completed application form along with your other documents.
4. Pay the applicable fee. Check current fees with the Department of Road Transport.
5. Hand over your original EU licence. This is collected as part of the process.
6. Wait for your Cypriot licence to be issued.

The Department processes applications at its offices around the island. ## What documents do you need to bring?

Getting this list right before you go saves you a wasted trip. The Department of Road Transport requires these items:

- Your valid EU driving licence (original, not a copy)
- Proof of Cyprus residence (your yellow slip or equivalent registration document)
- A recent passport-style photograph
- A completed application form (available at the Department office)

A few things to double-check before you go. Your EU licence must be valid. If it has expired, the process is more complicated (more on that below). Your proof of residence needs to be current. The yellow slip is the standard document most EU citizens receive when they register with the Civil Registry and Migration Department.

The application form itself is straightforward. You can pick it up at the office when you arrive, or in some cases download it in advance. Call ahead to confirm what is available.

Do not bring photocopies of your licence and expect them to be accepted. The original is required because it is physically collected as part of the exchange. Bring the real thing.

## What happens to your original EU licence?

This is the part that surprises many people. When you exchange your EU licence for a Cypriot one, your original licence is not simply cancelled and handed back to you. It is sent back to the authority that issued it in your home country.

You cannot keep both licences. You will not hold a German licence and a Cypriot licence at the same time. That is not how the EU mutual recognition system works. The idea is that each person holds one licence, issued by their country of residence.

This worries some people, especially those who travel back to their home country regularly. But there is nothing to worry about. Your Cypriot licence is an EU licence. It is valid in every EU member state. You can drive in your home country on your Cypriot licence exactly as you could on your original one.

If you later move away from Cyprus to another EU country, that country will recognise your Cypriot licence. The same mutual recognition rules apply throughout the EU.

The one thing to be aware of is that once you have submitted your original licence, you cannot get it back. If you change your mind after the exchange, there is no way to reverse it. So make sure you are ready before you hand it over. Most long-term residents have no regrets. But it is good to know what you are agreeing to.

## What is the deadline and what happens if you miss it?

Cyprus law sets a period after you become resident during which you can exchange your EU licence without any tests. Once that window closes, things can get more complicated.

The exact length of this period can change. The Department of Road Transport is the only reliable source for the current rule. Do not rely on forums or what worked for someone else a few years ago. Rules change. Confirm the deadline directly with the Department when you first register your residency.

If you miss the deadline, you may still be able to exchange, but there is a risk that tests could be required. That means theory and practical examinations, which most EU drivers naturally want to avoid. The best way to protect yourself is to start the exchange process early.

The Department has offices across the island, so you do not need to travel far. If you are busy or keep putting it off, set a reminder in your phone soon after you receive your yellow slip. One trip to the office is much better than having to sit a full driving test later.

## What if your EU licence has already expired?

This is where things get harder. If your EU licence expired before you applied for the exchange, the Department of Road Transport may not treat it as a straightforward swap. An expired licence is not a valid licence.

In this situation, the process can become more complicated and may require tests. Exactly what is required depends on the circumstances and the current rules at the time you apply. There is no single fixed answer. You need to speak directly with the Department.

Some people arrive in Cyprus with a licence that expired recently and assume the exchange will still go through easily. It may not. The Department will assess the situation and tell you what steps are needed.

If your licence expires while you are living here and you have not yet exchanged it, renew it with your home country first if possible. Then bring the valid, renewed licence to the Department for the exchange. Getting a renewal from another EU country while abroad is not always easy, but it is better than facing a test here.

If your licence was one of those issued without an expiry date, sometimes called lifetime licences, special rules apply. Some EU countries issued these in earlier decades. The Department treats them under separate rules. Check with them directly before you apply.

The safest approach is always to deal with the exchange while your licence is valid and within the window period. Letting either of those conditions lapse makes the whole thing harder.

Get your documents together, visit your nearest Department of Road Transport office, and get it done. Future you will appreciate not having to explain a foreign licence at a car rental desk or track down a replacement from another country.

## FAQ

**Do I have to exchange my EU licence if I live in Cyprus?**
You are not always legally required to exchange immediately, but a window period applies after you become resident. Within that period you can exchange without tests. After it closes, tests may be required. Check the current deadline with the Department of Road Transport.

**Can I drive in Cyprus on my home EU licence without exchanging it?**
Yes. EU and EEA licence holders living in Cyprus can drive legally on their home-country licence. Mutual recognition of EU driving licences applies in Cyprus as in all member states.

**Will I lose driving privileges in other EU countries after exchanging?**
No. A Cypriot licence is valid in all EU member states, so you can drive in your home country and anywhere else in the EU just as before. You simply present your Cypriot licence instead of your old one.

**What happens to my original licence when I exchange it?**
The Department of Road Transport sends your original licence back to the authority that issued it in your home country. You cannot keep both licences. You will hold only your new Cypriot one going forward.

**What if my EU licence expired before I applied for the exchange?**
An expired licence complicates the process and may require tests rather than a simple swap. Speak directly with the Department of Road Transport to find out what is needed in your specific situation. If possible, renew your licence in your home country before applying.

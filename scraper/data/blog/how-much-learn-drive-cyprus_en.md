Getting a driving licence in Cyprus is not cheap if you go in without a plan. There are more separate costs than most people expect, and some of them catch first-timers completely off guard. This guide breaks down every stage, so you and your family can budget properly from the start.

## What are the main costs involved in getting a driving licence in Cyprus?

Before you book a single lesson, it helps to see the full picture. Getting a Category B driving licence in Cyprus means moving through several distinct stages, and each one has a price tag.

Here is a summary of every cost category you will face:

- Learner permit (application and processing fee)
- Medical certificate from a registered doctor
- First aid course certificate
- Theory test preparation (sometimes called the code course)
- Theory test fee
- Practical driving lessons
- Practical driving test fee
- Final driving licence card fee

Some of these are government fees paid directly to the Department of Road Transport (TOM). Others go to your driving school, your doctor, or a first aid training provider. A few are one-off payments. Others, like the practical test fee, could repeat if you do not pass first time.

For a full walkthrough of the whole process, the [step-by-step driving licence guide](/en/blog/how-to-get-driving-licence-cyprus-step-by-step) covers each stage in order, from your first appointment to the day you collect your card.

The honest answer to "how much does it all cost?" is that it depends. Your city, your chosen school, and how quickly you learn all affect the final number. What this guide can do is make sure no cost comes as a surprise.

## How much does the learner permit and administration cost?

The first official step is applying for a Learner Permit, known in Greek as an Άδεια Μαθητευόμενου. You cannot legally drive on public roads in Cyprus without one, even with an instructor in the car.

You apply for the permit through the Department of Road Transport. The application involves submitting personal documents and paying a government processing fee. Check the current fee directly with TOM, as amounts can change with little public notice.

You will also need a medical certificate at this stage. A registered doctor must confirm that you are fit to drive. Because private doctors and clinics set their own rates, the price for this certificate varies quite a bit. Some clinics offer a basic driving medical for a lower fee, while others charge more for a full consultation. It pays to ring around before you book.

Additionally, you will need to provide passport photos and copies of your identity card or passport. These are small costs but worth budgeting for. Once your learner permit is approved, you are legally ready to start lessons on public roads.

The administrative side of getting a licence does not end with the learner permit. When you eventually pass all your tests, you pay another fee for the physical driving licence card itself. Again, check with TOM for the current amount before you budget.

## What do driving lessons cost in Cyprus?

Lessons are almost certainly your biggest single expense. Driving lesson prices in Cyprus are not fixed by law, so schools set their own rates. Hourly prices are generally higher in Nicosia and Limassol than in smaller towns like Paphos, Larnaca, or Paralimni.

For a realistic breakdown of what schools are currently charging, the [driving lesson costs in Cyprus](/en/blog/driving-lesson-cost-cyprus) page tracks current market rates by city.

Most schools offer two ways to pay:

- **Per lesson** - you pay each time, with no commitment up front.
- **Lesson packages** - you pay for a block of lessons at a discounted rate.

If you are confident you will need many lessons, a package usually works out cheaper per hour. But if you buy a large package and pass quickly, some schools may not refund unused sessions. Always ask about refund terms before you commit.

Cyprus law requires a minimum number of practical driving lessons before you are allowed to sit the practical test. The Department of Road Transport sets this minimum, so check with TOM for the current figure. In practice, most learners need more than the legal minimum before they feel ready.

Schools are regulated by the Department of Road Transport, which sets standards for instructor qualifications and the vehicles used for tuition. This means you are protected from the very worst operators, but quality and teaching style still vary widely between schools.

## How much do the theory and practical driving tests cost?

There are two separate tests, and both carry fees.

The theory test is computer-based. It tests your knowledge of traffic laws, road signs, and safe driving behaviour. You sit it at an approved centre. The fee is set by the Department of Road Transport, so check with TOM for the current amount. Most driving schools include a theory preparation course (μαθήματα κώδικα) in their package, but some charge for it separately. Ask your school directly before assuming it is included.

The practical driving test is the on-road assessment with an official examiner. If you pass, you move on to applying for your full licence card. If you fail, you pay a re-sit fee each time you rebook. That re-sit cost can add up fast if things do not go to plan, so it is worth being honest with your instructor about whether you are truly ready before you book the date.

For everything you need to know about what examiners look for and how the tests are structured, the [Cyprus driving test guide](/en/blog/cyprus-driving-test-2026) is a practical resource worth reading before you sit either test.

The practical test fee is paid to the Department of Road Transport. Check the current fee with TOM, as it may differ from what you find quoted on older websites or forums.

## What are the compulsory costs that candidates often overlook?

Two costs in particular seem to catch people off guard. Neither is optional for a Category B licence.

**First aid certificate**

You must hold a valid first aid certificate to get your driving licence in Cyprus. This means completing an approved first aid course, which has its own separate cost. The course is typically run by organisations like the Red Cross or St John Ambulance. Prices vary depending on the provider and the length of the course. Budget for this separately and book it early, as course dates fill up.

**Medical certificate**

As mentioned above, a doctor must certify you as medically fit to drive before your learner permit is issued. Because private practitioners set their own prices, you should ring several clinics to compare. Do not just walk into the nearest one.

**Theory preparation course**

Some schools treat the code course as part of their package. Others charge it as an add-on. Always confirm this in writing when you sign up. If you misread an "all-in" package and later find theory prep is extra, that is an unpleasant surprise.

**Travel and time costs**

These are not fees, but they are real. If your driving school is across town and you rely on a taxi or ride to get there, those trips add up. Choosing a school close to where you live or work is smarter than chasing a slightly lower lesson rate at a school far away.

The [complete Cyprus driving licence guide](/en/blog/cyprus-driving-licence-complete-guide) lists every requirement in one place, which helps you make sure you have not missed anything before you apply.

## How do you compare driving schools and find the best price?

Not all driving schools are equal, and price is only one part of the picture. A cheap school that books you in rarely and cancels often will cost you more in the long run. More lessons mean more money spent.

Here are the key things to check when comparing schools:

- What is the cost per lesson, and what is the package price?
- Is the theory preparation course included or charged separately?
- What is the cancellation and rescheduling policy?
- How soon can you get lesson slots each week?
- What vehicle will you be using?
- Is the instructor's style a good fit for you?

Word of mouth is still reliable in Cyprus. Ask friends, colleagues, or neighbours who passed recently. Online reviews help too, but take them with a pinch of salt.

Geography matters. If you are in the capital, look at [driving schools in Nicosia](/en/driving-schools/nicosia) to compare what is available locally. If you are based on the coast, [driving schools in Limassol](/en/driving-schools/limassol) lists options in the city with the highest lesson rates in the south. Choosing a school close to home can reduce travel costs and make it easier to book consistent, regular lessons.

Regular lessons, booked close together, tend to produce faster progress. A learner who drives twice a week moves faster than one who drives twice a month. Faster progress means fewer total lessons, and fewer lessons means a lower final bill.

## How can you keep your total costs as low as possible?

There is no magic trick here. But there are sensible habits that keep costs under control.

**Study hard for the theory test before you sit it.** A failed theory test costs you a re-sit fee and delays your practical lessons. Use the practice questions available through your school and online. Do not book the theory test until you are consistently scoring well in mock tests.

**Be honest with your instructor before booking the practical test.** Failed practical tests are expensive. Re-sit fees add up, and you may also need extra lessons before the next attempt. If your instructor says you are not ready, listen.

**Book a package deal carefully.** Packages save money per lesson, but only if you use all the sessions. Ask what happens to unused lessons if you pass early or switch schools.

**Do not delay between stages.** Long gaps between lessons mean you forget things. Forgetting things means more lessons to get back to the same level. Consistent weekly lessons are almost always cheaper overall than an on-again, off-again approach.

**Compare medical certificate prices.** Ring two or three clinics. The variation can be meaningful.

**Book the first aid course early.** Leaving it late can delay your licence application if no slots are available.

Small savings at each stage add up. A learner who studies well, books lessons regularly, and passes tests first time will spend noticeably less than one who drifts through the process without a plan.

## FAQ

**What is the cheapest way to get a driving licence in Cyprus?**
The cheapest route is to study thoroughly before your theory test, pass both tests first time, and book lessons in a regular block so you do not lose progress between sessions. Choosing a school close to home also cuts travel costs. Packages typically cost less per lesson than paying individually, provided you use all the sessions you pay for.

**Do driving lesson prices in Cyprus include the theory test preparation?**
It depends on the school. Some driving schools in Cyprus bundle theory preparation (μαθήματα κώδικα) into their lesson packages, while others charge for it as a separate item. Always ask for a written breakdown of what is included before you pay anything.

**How many driving lessons will I need before I can take the practical test in Cyprus?**
Cyprus law sets a minimum number of lessons before a candidate may sit the practical test, but most learners need more than the legal minimum before they are genuinely ready. The Department of Road Transport (TOM) holds the current minimum figure, and your instructor will give you an honest assessment of where you stand.

**Can I fail the theory test and how much does a re-sit cost?**
Yes, you can fail the theory test in Cyprus. If you do, you must pay a re-sit fee and wait before booking again. The exact fee is set by the Department of Road Transport, so check with TOM for the current amount. Thorough preparation before your first attempt is the best way to avoid this extra cost.

**Is the first aid certificate really compulsory for a Cyprus driving licence?**
Yes. A valid first aid certificate is a legal requirement for obtaining a Category B driving licence in the Republic of Cyprus. You must complete an approved first aid course and present the certificate as part of your licence application. There is a separate fee for the course, which varies by provider.

Three schools is a short list. That has a good side and a bad side. You can phone all of them in one afternoon. But you cannot easily walk away from a school you dislike without travelling further.

If you want a shortlist that is already ranked, see our page on the [best driving schools in Paralimni](/en/best-driving-schools-paralimni). Use it as a starting point. A ranking cannot tell you whether an instructor suits you.

Every driving school in Cyprus must be licensed by the Department of Road Transport. In Greek this is the Τμήμα Οδικών Μεταφορών, or ΤΟΜ. Instructors also hold an instructor licence from ΤΟΜ. These licences are the legal minimum. If you are unsure, ask to see proof.

Here is a simple way to compare a small group of schools:

- Phone every school on the same day and ask the same questions.
- Write down what each one says about price, language and lesson times.
- Book a first lesson with your top two before you buy a package.
- Notice how fast each school replies. It shows how they will treat you later.

Schools can change over time. Some close and new ones open. So treat any list as a guide, and confirm by phone that a school is still taking students.

## Why does the language of your lessons matter here?

This area has many foreign residents and seasonal workers. Not everyone speaks Greek well. Lessons in a language you only half understand are slow and tiring. You may nod at an instruction and then miss what it meant.

Many schools in Cyprus teach in Greek and English. Some also offer other languages, such as Russian or Arabic. Do not assume that a school here offers yours. Ask the question directly.

There are three places where language matters:

- **In the car.** The instructor gives quick orders about mirrors, signals and gears. You must follow them at once.
- **In the theory course.** The theory test covers road signs, traffic rules and safe driving. You can sit it in Greek or English.
- **On the paperwork.** Forms may be in Greek. Ask whether the school helps you fill them in.

Ask who will actually teach you. A friendly receptionist who speaks your language is not enough. The instructor in the passenger seat is the person who counts. Some schools match students to instructors by language. Ask if yours does.

If you are new to Cyprus, the steps for foreigners have a few extras. Our guide on [getting a driving licence in Cyprus as a foreigner](/en/blog/how-to-get-driving-licence-cyprus-foreigner) explains them. Read it before you phone any school. You will ask better questions.

Cyprus also drives on the left. If you learned to drive elsewhere, your instructor must explain this clearly from the first lesson. Good communication makes that change much easier.

## Are you eligible to start learning to drive?

Check this before you book any lessons. Cyprus issues licences to people who are legally resident. If you are an EU citizen, your EU registration certificate or Cyprus residence document confirms your status. If you are from outside the EU, you need a valid residence permit from the Migration Department. A tourist visa is not enough.

Age matters too. You can apply for the provisional licence from age 17. A full Category B car licence needs you to be 18. A parent can help a 17-year-old with the process, but the applicant goes to the office in person.

Our guide to the [provisional driving licence in Cyprus](/en/blog/cyprus-provisional-driving-licence) lists the papers you need. In short, you bring:

- an identity card or passport
- a medical certificate from a doctor
- an eye examination result
- two passport-style photos
- the completed application form

## What is the route from first document to full licence?

The route is the same in Paralimni as in every other town in Cyprus. Only the logistics change. Here are the steps in order:

1. Prove that you are a legal resident and gather your documents.
2. Get your medical certificate and eye test result.
3. Apply for the provisional licence in person.
4. Take lessons with a licensed school in a dual-control car.
5. Pass the theory test, then the practical test.

The provisional licence is called the εκπαιδευτική άδεια. You need it before any registered school can take you out on the road. It lets you drive only in a dual-control car with a registered instructor. You may not drive alone on it.

The theory test is done on a computer. You can take it in Greek or English. It covers road signs, traffic rules and safe driving behaviour. You cannot sit the practical test until you pass the theory test.

Once you pass the theory test, your driving school books the practical test for you. You do not book it yourself. The practical test takes place on public roads. A ΤΟΜ examiner sits in the passenger seat. You drive the school's dual-control car.

Ask your school how long a theory result stays valid. You do not want to wait so long that you must sit it again. The [Cyprus driving test guide](/en/blog/cyprus-driving-test-2026) explains both tests in detail.

## What should you ask a school before you book?

A short phone call can save you weeks and money. In a town with only a few schools, you should still ask every question. Do not feel shy about it. Good schools expect these questions and answer them calmly.

Here is a list of questions to ask:

- Is the school licensed by the Department of Road Transport?
- Will my instructor speak my language?
- What exactly does the quoted price include?
- Are the theory lessons included, or do they cost extra?
- Is the first aid course included? It is a compulsory part of the process, so confirm current rules with ΤΟΜ.
- What happens if I need more lessons than the package covers?
- What happens to unused lessons if I pass early or change school?
- Can I book lessons around my work shifts?
- Does the school book my practical test for me?

Write the answers down. Then compare them with the other schools. Be careful with prices said out loud on the phone. Ask for them in writing.

Never pay for a large package on the first visit. Take one lesson first. Treat it like an interview. Watch how the instructor corrects your mistakes. Good correction builds confidence. Harsh correction makes you tense and slows you down.

There is no need to pick the cheapest school. A cheap package that covers too few lessons can end up costing more. A school that explains its prices clearly is usually a better bet. Ask for a fair, clear list of what you will pay for and when.

## How do pick-ups, travel and practice routes work in a smaller area?

In a small town, the small details decide whether lessons fit your life. Ask where your lessons start. Some instructors collect you from home or work. Others use a fixed meeting point. Ayia Napa and Paralimni are close, but your daily routine may still make a difference.

Ask how long each lesson lasts and how often you can book. Regular lessons work better than long gaps. If you work in a resort, your free time may change with the season. Choose a school that can change your slots without a fuss.

Ask where the practical test takes place. Ask whether your school drives there for lessons, so you know those roads. Familiar roads help on test day. Some schools plan routes with this in mind. Others drive wherever is easiest.

Ask which ΤΟΜ district office handles your application and your appointments. Do not guess. Check with the Department of Road Transport before you travel, and ask your school what it advises. A wasted trip costs you half a day.

Your instructor should also teach you the roads you will really use. In a resort area, that means busy junctions and roundabouts. Cyprus drives on the left. Roundabouts can feel odd at first if you learned elsewhere. Ask your instructor to practise them often.

## How do you compare schools and decide when to start?

Put your notes next to each other. Look at language, lesson times, pick-up, and what the price includes. Then choose the two schools you liked most. Book one lesson with each. One trial lesson is a small step compared with a package you later regret.

Think about your budget in stages. You will pay for the provisional licence, a medical certificate and an eye test. Then come lessons, both tests and a first aid course. Some fees go to ΤΟΜ. Others go to your school or a doctor. Ask each school to list what it charges for and what it does not.

Think about your calendar too. Seasonal work can fill your days in summer. Plan your lessons before your busiest months begin. If you must pause, tell your school early. Long gaps make you forget what you learned.

Start the paperwork first. The documents take time to collect. Schools can fill up, so book your lessons as soon as your provisional licence arrives. Do not wait until the last minute before a deadline.

Finally, remember that a school can only help as much as you practise. Ask your instructor for clear goals after each lesson. Ask what you should work on next. A good school tells you honestly when you are ready for the test, and when you are not.

Choosing a school in Paralimni or Ayia Napa takes a few phone calls and an hour of thought. Start by checking your eligibility. Then compare the schools on language, logistics and what the price includes. Take a first lesson before you commit. Confirm every fee with the Department of Road Transport. With a clear plan, you can move from your first document to your full licence with fewer surprises.

## FAQ

**Are there driving schools in Ayia Napa itself?**
Our site lists its schools for this area under Paralimni, and we do not list a separate school for Ayia Napa. Phone the schools in Paralimni and ask whether they collect learners from Ayia Napa. Confirm this before you book.

**Can I take the theory test in English?**
Yes. The theory test is computer-based, and you can choose Greek or English. Tell your school which language you want so that your lessons and practice material match it.

**Who books my practical test?**
After you pass the theory test, your driving school books the practical test for you. You do not book it yourself. Ask your school how long the wait usually is and what you should bring.

**Can I drive alone once I have the provisional licence?**
No. The provisional licence lets you drive only in a dual-control car with a registered instructor. Driving alone on it is against the law. Wait until you pass both tests and hold your full licence.

**Where can I check the current fees?**
Check them with the Department of Road Transport. Fees can change, and schools sometimes quote old figures. Ask each school to separate its own charges from the government fees, so you can compare them fairly.

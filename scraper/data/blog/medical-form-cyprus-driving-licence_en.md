If you are applying for a driving licence in Cyprus, paperwork is a big part of the process. One document that trips people up is the medical certificate from a pathologist. It sounds more serious than it usually is, but you do need to know what it is, who can sign it, and when you actually need one.

## What is the pathologist's medical certificate for a driving licence?

The medical certificate is an official form used by the Department of Road Transport (ΤΟΜ) to confirm that you are medically fit to drive. In Cyprus, the relevant form is known as ΤΟΜ 62 or an equivalent approved form. The pathologist fills it in after examining you, then signs and stamps it.

The purpose is straightforward. Cyprus, like all EU member states, follows the medical standards set out in EU Directive 2006/126/EC. This directive lists the health conditions that can affect a person's ability to drive safely. Cyprus has written those rules into its own law. The medical certificate is how the system checks that you meet those standards before handing you a licence.

The certificate does not say you are in perfect health. It says you do not have a condition that would make driving unsafe. There is a difference. A person can have a managed chronic condition and still be cleared to drive, as long as that condition does not impair their driving ability.

The form itself is not something you download and fill in yourself. The pathologist completes the clinical sections. You provide your personal details and sign where required. ΤΟΜ will not accept a certificate that is missing the doctor's registration details, signature, or stamp.

## Which doctor can sign the certificate?

Only a pathologist (παθολόγος) registered with the Cyprus Medical Council can sign the medical certificate for a Cyprus driving licence. A general practitioner with a different specialty, or a doctor licensed in another country but not registered in Cyprus, cannot sign it.

The good news is that you have a wide choice of doctors. You do not need to go to a government hospital. Private pathologists registered in Cyprus can issue the certificate just as validly as a doctor working in a public clinic. Most private pathologist offices in Nicosia, Limassol, Larnaca, Paphos, and Famagusta can do this. Many are used to it and keep the correct forms in stock.

When you book your appointment, tell the receptionist that you need a medical certificate for a driving licence (πιστοποιητικό για δίπλωμα οδήγησης). This helps them prepare the right paperwork before you arrive. Some clinics charge a separate fee for completing official forms, so ask about this when you call.

The pathologist must include their Cyprus Medical Council registration number on the form. ΤΟΜ checks this. If the number is missing or does not match, your application will be held up. Always check the form before you leave the clinic.

## When do you need the medical certificate?

Not everyone applying for or renewing a Cyprus licence will need a medical certificate. But several groups definitely do.

**First-time applicants for Category B:** If you are applying for a standard car licence (Category B) for the first time in Cyprus, you must include a pathologist's certificate in your application package. There are no exceptions to this.

**Older drivers renewing their licence:** Drivers above a certain age threshold must submit a fresh medical certificate when they renew. ΤΟΜ's rules on the exact age cut-off can change, so check the current threshold directly with the Department of Road Transport before your renewal date.

**Drivers applying for higher categories:** If you are applying for a Category C, D, CE, or DE licence (trucks, buses, or vehicles with heavy trailers), a more detailed medical examination is required. The standard pathologist certificate may not be enough on its own for these categories.

**Licence holders with certain medical conditions:** If ΤΟΜ has a note on your file about a health condition, they may ask for an updated certificate even outside the normal renewal cycle.

## What does the pathologist check during the examination?

The examination is usually straightforward for most applicants. The pathologist is checking whether you have any condition listed under EU Directive 2006/126/EC that would disqualify you from holding a driving licence or require restrictions to be placed on it.

Key areas the doctor will assess include:

- Vision: whether your eyesight meets the minimum standard for driving, with or without glasses or contact lenses
- Neurological health: conditions such as epilepsy, where uncontrolled seizures would make driving unsafe
- Cardiovascular health: serious heart conditions that could cause sudden loss of consciousness
- Mental health: severe conditions that could affect judgment or awareness while driving
- Musculoskeletal function: whether you have the physical ability to control a vehicle safely
- Diabetes and other metabolic conditions: particularly cases where hypoglycaemia could cause sudden incapacity

The pathologist will ask about your medical history and any current medications. Be honest. Providing false information on a medical certificate is a legal matter, not just an administrative one.

The appointment is typically short - often 20 to 30 minutes - unless the doctor needs more time to review your records. You do not need to fast beforehand or bring medical equipment. Just bring your ID and any relevant medical records if you have a known condition.

## What happens if the doctor identifies a health issue?

Most people pass the medical examination without any problems. But sometimes the pathologist finds something that needs a closer look. This does not automatically mean you cannot get a licence.

If the doctor identifies a condition that might affect your driving, they may decline to sign the certificate until you get further tests or a specialist opinion. For example, if your blood pressure is very high on the day, they might ask you to come back after treatment.

In other cases, the pathologist signs the certificate but adds a note. This note might recommend restrictions, such as requiring you to wear corrective lenses while driving. ΤΟΜ can then issue a licence with that restriction coded on it.

If ΤΟΜ receives a certificate with concerns noted, or if they have reason to question your fitness to drive, they can refer you for further specialist assessment. This is a formal step in the process. The specialist's report then goes back to ΤΟΜ, and they make a decision based on the full picture.

In serious cases, where a condition is found that clearly disqualifies driving under EU standards, a licence will not be issued until the condition is managed or resolved. This is not common, but it does happen. The rules exist to protect everyone on the road.

## Where do you submit the certificate and what are the next steps?

Once you have the signed and stamped certificate from your pathologist, you submit it to ΤΟΜ (the Department of Road Transport) as part of your full application package. ΤΟΜ has district offices in Nicosia, Limassol, Larnaca, Paphos, and other locations. You submit in person.

The medical certificate does not go in on its own. It is one part of a package that includes your identity documents, your application form, photos, and any other documents specific to your category or situation. ΤΟΜ reviews the full package together.

After submission, ΤΟΜ processes your application. If everything is in order, they will confirm your eligibility to proceed. For first-time applicants, the next steps typically include a theory test and a practical driving test. The [Cyprus driving test guide](/en/blog/cyprus-driving-test-2026) covers what to expect at both stages.

For renewals where only the medical certificate is required (common for older drivers), the process is simpler. You submit the documents and ΤΟΜ issues the renewed licence once they are satisfied.

Check current fees with the Department of Road Transport, as costs can change. ΤΟΜ's official website and district offices are the most reliable source for up-to-date fee information and any changes to required documents.

The whole process moves faster when your paperwork is complete and correct on the first visit. Double-check every form before you go, and bring copies of everything.

It is not complicated, but it does need the right doctor and the right form. Once you have it, you are one solid step closer to holding a valid Cyprus driving licence. ## FAQ

**What is Form ΤΟΜ 62 and where do I get it?**
Form ΤΟΜ 62 is the official medical certificate form used for Cyprus driving licence applications. Many pathologist clinics keep it in stock because it is a common request. You can also ask ΤΟΜ directly for the current version of the form before your appointment.

**Can my family doctor sign the medical certificate for my driving licence?**
Only if your family doctor is a registered pathologist (παθολόγος) with the Cyprus Medical Council. A doctor with a different specialty cannot sign the form, even if they know your full medical history. Always confirm the doctor's specialty before booking.

**How long is the medical certificate valid for?**
ΤΟΜ sets a limit on how old the certificate can be when you submit it. This timeframe can change, so check the current accepted period directly with the Department of Road Transport before booking your appointment. Submitting an expired certificate will delay your application.

**Do I need a new medical certificate every time I renew my licence?**
Not always. Whether you need one depends on your age and your licence category. Drivers above a certain age threshold and holders of higher category licences are most likely to need a fresh certificate at renewal. Check current rules with ΤΟΜ, as the age thresholds can be updated.

**What if I wear glasses - will that stop me from getting a licence?**
No. Wearing glasses or contact lenses does not disqualify you from driving. If your vision meets the required standard with corrective lenses, the pathologist can certify you as fit. Your licence will then be issued with a code showing that you must wear corrective lenses while driving.

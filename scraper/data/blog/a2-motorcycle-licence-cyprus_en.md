The A2 licence in Cyprus lets you ride motorcycles up to 35 kW from the age of 18. After holding the A2 for at least two years you can upgrade to a full Category A licence by passing an additional practical test. All training must be completed at a driving school licensed by the Department of Road Transport (TOM).

Getting a motorcycle licence in Cyprus is more straightforward than many people think. But the rules matter, and getting them wrong can cost you time and money. This guide covers everything you need to know about the A2 licence, from the bike limits to the test itself, and what comes next if you want to ride anything you like.

## What Is the A2 Licence and Who Is It For?

The A2 is a category of motorcycle licence that sits in the middle of Cyprus's progressive licensing system. It sits above the A1 (for smaller bikes) and below the full Category A. If you want to understand the full picture of how licence categories fit together, the [Cyprus driving licence categories](/en/blog/cyprus-driving-licence-categories) page gives a clear breakdown.

Cyprus follows EU Directive 2006/126/EC. This brings Cypriot rules in line with the rest of Europe. That matters if you plan to ride in other EU countries. Your A2 licence issued in Cyprus will be recognised across the European Union.

The A2 is aimed at riders aged 18 to 23 who are ready to move beyond small bikes. It is also a stepping stone. You do not jump straight to unrestricted riding. The system is designed to build experience gradually before you handle more powerful machines.

If you have never held any motorcycle licence before, the A2 is likely your starting point at 18. If you have already held an A1 licence, you can move up to A2 once you meet the age and holding-period requirements. Either way, you will go through proper training and sit both a theory and a practical test.

The A2 is not a shortcut. It is a structured qualification. Riders who earn it have demonstrated they can handle a mid-range motorcycle safely on public roads, including Cyprus roads, where traffic, road surfaces, and summer heat all add to the challenge.

## What Is the Minimum Age and What Are the Basic Requirements?

The minimum age to apply for an A2 licence in Cyprus is 18 years old. You must have reached that age before you can sit the practical test.

Beyond age, here is what you need:

- A valid identity document or passport
- A valid Cyprus residence address (for non-citizens resident in Cyprus)
- A medical fitness certificate if required by the Department of Road Transport
- Completion of mandatory basic training (MBT) at a licensed driving school

One common question is whether holding a Category B (car) licence speeds things up. The short answer is: a little, but not as much as you might hope. Holding a B licence does not exempt you from the motorcycle theory test or the practical test. You still need to pass both. What it does show is that you already understand general road rules, which can make the theory test easier to prepare for.

For anyone starting from scratch with no driving licence at all, the process is the same. You complete your MBT, sit the theory test, and then sit the practical test. The [step-by-step driving licence guide](/en/blog/how-to-get-driving-licence-cyprus-step-by-step) walks through the general process in more detail and is worth reading before you book anything.

Cyprus drives on the left. If you have previously ridden or driven in a country where traffic flows on the right, this is something to take seriously during training. Good driving schools will factor this into how they teach you.

## Which Motorcycles Are Permitted on an A2 Licence in Cyprus?

![Motorcycle rider on a sunny Cyprus road, A2 licence restrictions](/blog/a2-motorcycle-licence-cyprus/inline-1.jpg)
*A rider on a Cyprus road - power limits apply for all A2 licence holders, via Pexels.com*

This is where many new riders trip up, so read this section carefully.

On an A2 licence in Cyprus, your motorcycle must meet both of the following limits:

- Maximum power output: 35 kW (roughly 47 bhp)
- Maximum power-to-weight ratio: 0.2 kW per kilogram

Both limits must be met at the same time. A bike that passes one but fails the other is not legal for an A2 rider.

There is also a rule about de-restricted bikes. Some manufacturers sell higher-powered motorcycles with a restrictor fitted to bring them within A2 limits. Cyprus law allows this, but only up to a point. You cannot ride a bike that has been restricted down from a model that originally produced more than 70 kW. So if a bike's unrestricted version makes more than 70 kW, fitting a restrictor does not make it legal for A2 use in Cyprus.

This rule catches a lot of people out. Always check the unrestricted power output of the base model, not just the restricted version you are looking at buying. Ask the dealer for written confirmation. If in doubt, contact the Department of Road Transport directly before you buy.

Popular A2-compliant bikes tend to be mid-range nakeds, adventure bikes, and sports models from manufacturers like Honda, Kawasaki, Yamaha, and Triumph, among others. There are plenty of good options. You do not need to feel limited.

## How Is A2 Training and Testing Structured in Cyprus?

![Young adult on a motorcycle with an instructor during training in Cyprus](/blog/a2-motorcycle-licence-cyprus/inline-2.jpg)
*A learner and instructor during motorcycle training - all A2 candidates must complete mandatory basic training, via Pexels.com*

Training for the A2 licence in Cyprus follows a set structure. You cannot simply book a test and turn up. The process goes in order.

Here is how it works, step by step:

1. Enrol at a driving school licensed by the Department of Road Transport (TOM).
2. Complete mandatory basic training (MBT). This covers slow-speed control, emergency stops, and basic road riding.
3. Sit the motorcycle theory test at TOM. This is a written or computer-based test covering road rules, hazard awareness, and motorcycle-specific knowledge.
4. Once you pass the theory test, you are eligible to sit the practical test.
5. Pass the practical test, which includes both an off-road manoeuvres section and an on-road riding assessment.

The practical test in Cyprus takes place on public roads. Because Cyprus drives on the left, test routes will involve left-hand traffic. Your instructor will prepare you for this during MBT.

The theory test is conducted by TOM. You will be tested on Cyprus road rules and general motorcycle safety. Your driving school will usually guide you on how to prepare, and many schools include theory preparation as part of their course.

There is no set national minimum number of training hours published for the A2, but your school must complete MBT to TOM standards before certifying you ready for the practical test. A good school will tell you honestly how many sessions you are likely to need.

For more on what the test itself involves, the [Cyprus driving test guide](/en/blog/cyprus-driving-test-2026) covers the practical test format in useful detail.

## How Much Does It Cost to Get an A2 Licence?

Costs for getting an A2 licence in Cyprus come from several sources. It is worth understanding each one so you are not surprised.

The main cost areas are:

- Theory test fee (paid to TOM)
- Practical test fee (paid to TOM)
- Driving school training fees
- Any medical certificate fees if applicable
- Licence issue fee

TOM fees are set by the government and can change. Always check current fees with the Department of Road Transport before you budget. Do not rely on figures you find online, including anything in this article.

Driving school fees vary. They depend on the school, the location, the number of sessions you need, and whether theory preparation is included. Schools in cities like Nicosia tend to have more competitive pricing because there are more options. For a fuller sense of what training costs in Cyprus generally, the [driving lesson costs in Cyprus](/en/blog/driving-lesson-cost-cyprus) guide is a useful reference point.

A rough planning tip: budget for the test fees, a full course of training, and a buffer in case you need extra lessons or a re-test. Most candidates pass first time with proper preparation, but it is better to plan for the realistic scenario than the best-case one.

Some schools offer package deals that bundle MBT, theory prep, and test preparation into one price. These can offer good value, but compare what is actually included before you sign up.

## How Do You Upgrade from A2 to a Full Category A Licence?

This is the part that A2 holders in Cyprus often start thinking about around year one, once the freedom of riding starts to feel familiar and the power limit starts to feel like a ceiling.

The rules are clear. After holding your A2 licence for at least two years, you are eligible to apply for the full Category A licence. The full A has no power restrictions at all. You can ride any motorcycle, regardless of engine size or power output.

The upgrade process depends on your age at the time you apply:

- If you are under 24, you must pass an additional practical test. No new theory test is required.
- If you are 24 or over, you still take the practical test. The theory requirement does not apply.

In both cases, you will need to train at a licensed school before sitting the practical test. This is not optional. The test itself is more demanding than the A2 practical. Examiners expect you to show higher-level control, especially at speed and in more complex traffic situations.

The full Category A licence is a significant step up. Many riders find it helpful to take a short refresher course even if they feel confident. A good instructor will identify any habits that formed during A2 riding and help you correct them before test day.

## How to Choose the Right Motorcycle School in Cyprus?

Not all driving schools are the same. Choosing the right one can make a real difference to how quickly you progress and how confident you feel on test day.

Start with the basics. Any school you consider must be officially licensed by the Department of Road Transport. This is not a nice-to-have. It is a legal requirement. An unlicensed school cannot certify your MBT, and without that certification, you cannot sit the practical test.

Beyond licensing, here is what to look for:

- Instructors with motorcycle-specific qualifications, not just general driving instruction experience
- Training bikes that are A2-compliant and well maintained
- Clear communication about how many sessions you will likely need
- A school that is honest about your readiness for the test, not one that rushes you through
- Good reviews from previous motorcycle students specifically

Location matters too. If you are based in Nicosia, it makes sense to train in the city where you will likely take your test. The [driving schools in Nicosia](/en/driving-schools/nicosia) directory is a good starting point for finding options near you.

Ask each school directly: how long is a typical A2 course for someone at my experience level? A school that gives you a straight, honest answer is one you can trust.

Also ask whether they offer upgrade training from A2 to A. If you are planning to progress eventually, starting with a school that can take you all the way makes the whole journey smoother.

The A2 licence opens up a real range of riding in Cyprus. Done properly, with the right school and the right preparation, it is an achievable goal for any motivated adult at 18. The two-year waiting period before you can go for the full A can feel long when you are at the start, but most riders look back and see it as time well spent on building real-world skills. Take the training seriously, choose a school you trust, and the road ahead is a good one.

## FAQ

**Can I ride any motorcycle in Cyprus with an A2 licence?**
No. Your bike must produce no more than 35 kW and must not exceed a power-to-weight ratio of 0.2 kW per kilogram. A bike that has been restricted down from a model producing more than 70 kW in its original form is also not permitted on an A2 licence in Cyprus.

**Does holding a car (Category B) licence in Cyprus mean I skip any motorcycle tests?**
No. Holding a Category B licence does not exempt you from the motorcycle theory test or the practical test. You still need to complete mandatory basic training and pass both tests to get your A2 licence.

**How long does the A2 training process take in Cyprus?**
It depends on your starting experience and how quickly you progress through training. Most candidates complete MBT, theory preparation, and practical test preparation over several weeks. Your driving school can give you a more specific estimate based on your background.

**What happens if I fail the A2 practical test in Cyprus?**
You can re-sit the practical test after a waiting period. Your driving school will advise you on next steps, including whether extra training sessions are recommended before you re-test. Check current re-test fees directly with the Department of Road Transport.

**Can I use my Cyprus A2 licence to ride motorcycles in other EU countries?**
Yes. Because Cyprus follows EU Directive 2006/126/EC, your A2 licence is recognised across EU member states. The same bike restrictions apply - 35 kW maximum and the power-to-weight ratio limit - wherever you ride within the EU.

## How does the licence process work for Larnaca learners?

The rules come from the Department of Road Transport, known as ΤΟΜ. They are the same wherever you live in the Republic of Cyprus. Larnaca has its own district office, so you do not need to travel to another city to start.

Here is the usual route for a Category B car licence:

1. Get a medical certificate from a doctor and complete an eye test.
2. Apply in person for your learner's licence at the ΤΟΜ district office.
3. Take lessons with a licensed driving school in a dual-control car, with a registered instructor beside you.
4. Pass the computer-based theory test. You can sit it in Greek or English.
5. Pass the practical test on public roads with a ΤΟΜ examiner. Your school books this test for you once you have passed the theory.
6. Receive your full Category B licence.

The learner's licence is called the εκπαιδευτική άδεια in Greek. You need it before your first lesson. You can apply from the age of 17. Bring your ID or passport, your medical certificate, your eye test result, and passport photos.

Call the Larnaca office before you go and confirm exactly what to bring. Practice can vary slightly between offices. Fees are set by ΤΟΜ and can change. Check the current amounts with them directly, not with a figure you saw online.

## How do you compare Larnaca driving schools?

Start with the basics. Every driving school in Cyprus must be licensed by ΤΟΜ, and every instructor must hold an instructor licence. Ask to see both. A good school will not mind the question.

Next, compare like with like. One school may quote a low price per lesson. Another may quote a package that includes theory lessons and test preparation. The numbers are not comparable until you know what each one covers. Write down what is included for every school you call.

You will not find a public table of pass rates for each school. ΤΟΜ does not publish one. If a school quotes you a pass rate, ask how it is counted. Is it first attempt or overall? How many students does it cover? Treat the figure as one signal among several.

Distance matters more than people expect. Lessons work best when they are regular. If the school is a hassle to reach, you will cancel more often and progress more slowly. Pick a school that is easy to get to from your home or workplace.

## Which questions should you ask before you book?

A short phone call can save you weeks of frustration. Have your questions ready before you dial. Write the answers down, because after three calls the schools start to blur together.

These are the questions worth asking every school:

- Which languages do your instructors teach in?
- Are lessons in a manual or an automatic car?
- How long is one lesson, and how often can I book?
- Will the instructor pick me up from home or work?
- Who provides the car on test day?
- Is it cheaper to pay per lesson or to buy a package?
- What happens if I cancel or need to rebook a lesson?

Pay attention to how the person answers, not just what they say. A confident school has heard these questions many times. It will answer clearly and without irritation. A school that rushes you or avoids a direct answer is telling you something too.

Ask about extras in plain terms. Is the theory course included or separate? Is the first aid course included? Ask for the answer in writing. Some items may be paid to the school, and others go to ΤΟΜ, a doctor or a training provider.

If you hold a licence from another country, say so at the start. The school can advise you on your options. Then confirm the rules with ΤΟΜ.

## Does the language of your lessons matter?

For newcomers, language may be the most important question of all. Technical instructions are quick. Check your mirror. Signal now. Slow down for the roundabout. If you miss a word, you miss the instruction.

Many driving schools in Cyprus teach in both Greek and English. Some also offer other languages, such as Russian or Arabic. Larnaca is an international area, so it is worth asking directly which languages each instructor speaks. Do not assume that English on the website means English in the car.

Think about the theory side too. The theory test is computer-based and you can take it in Greek or English. But your preparation matters as much as the test itself. Ask whether the school provides study material in your language. Ask whether it uses practice tests that look like the real thing.

A good instructor does more than translate. They explain why a rule exists. Cyprus drives on the left, which is a big change if you learned on the right. An instructor who understands this can coach you through the first hours more calmly.

## What should you check about the car, the lessons and test day?

Learner lessons take place in a dual-control car. That means the instructor has a brake pedal on their side. It lets you try new skills with less fear. Ask the school how old its cars are and how they are kept.

Ask whether the lesson car is manual or automatic. Then ask what that choice means for the licence you receive at the end. Do not rely on the school alone for this. Confirm it with ΤΟΜ before you book a package, because the answer affects what you can drive later.

Lesson length and timing matter. Some schools offer short slots in the working day. Others offer evenings and weekends, which often fill first. If you work full time, ask early about your preferred hours.

Pick-up is another practical point. Some instructors collect you from home, work or school. That saves time, and it means you start each lesson relaxed. Others expect you to meet them at the school. Neither is wrong, but it should fit your week.

Test day needs a plan as well. After you pass the theory test, your school books the practical test for you. Ask who provides the car on the day, and whether you will practise in it first. Ask whether your lessons cover the kind of roads used for the test.

## How do you decide and get started?

You now have enough to make a choice. Do not spend weeks comparing. Three good phone calls are usually enough. Follow these steps:

1. Open the Larnaca listing and pick three schools that are easy to reach.
2. Call each one and ask the questions from this guide.
3. Ask for a written quote that lists what is included.
4. Book one trial lesson with your favourite.
5. Start your learner's licence paperwork at the ΤΟΜ office at the same time.

It helps to begin the paperwork early. You need your learner's licence before any lessons. The medical certificate and eye test take a little time to arrange. Many people lose a week or two simply waiting for a doctor's appointment.

Set a budget with room to spare. There is no legal minimum number of lessons for a Category B licence. Some people need fewer lessons, and others need more. A plan that allows for extra lessons is less stressful than one that assumes you will pass first time.

Stay patient with yourself. The whole path from learner's licence to full licence usually takes several months. Regular lessons and steady practice matter more than speed.

Choosing a school in Larnaca does not need to be complicated. Make a shortlist, ask direct questions, take one trial lesson, and trust what you notice. The right school will feel clear and calm from the first phone call. Start the paperwork now, so your first lesson is ready when you are.

## What Does the Cyprus Driving Examiner Assess?

The practical driving test in Cyprus is run by the Department of Road Transport (ΤΟΜ), which sits under the Ministry of Transport, Communications and Works. The examiner travels with you for around 30 to 40 minutes on real public roads. They are not there to trick you. They are watching whether you can drive safely and independently.

The examiner uses a standardised marking sheet. Every action you take behind the wheel is judged against a fixed set of criteria. You can pick up minor faults and still pass. But one serious or dangerous fault ends the test immediately, even if everything else was fine.

Here is what the examiner is watching throughout the drive:

- **Observation** - Do you check mirrors at the right moments? Do you look into junctions properly?
- **Signalling** - Do you signal early enough and cancel signals after turning?
- **Road position** - Are you in the correct lane? Do you keep left on normal roads?
- **Speed** - Are you within the limit? Are you going too slowly for the conditions?
- **Give way rules** - Do you yield correctly at junctions, crossings, and roundabouts?
- **Vehicle control** - Is your steering, braking, and gear use smooth?
- **Awareness** - Do you spot hazards early and react sensibly?

For a full breakdown of what the test involves, read this [Cyprus driving test guide](/en/blog/cyprus-driving-test-2026). It is worth understanding the format before you book your appointment.

Cyprus drives on the left, which is rare in the EU. That affects how you position the car, how you turn, and how you use roundabouts. If you learned to drive in a right-hand-traffic country before moving here, give yourself extra time to adjust.

## What Are the Most Common Mistakes That Cause Failures?

![Examiner sitting in the passenger seat of a test car in Cyprus, holding a clipboard during a practical driving assessment](/blog/pass-driving-test-cyprus-first-time/inline-1.jpg)
*An examiner observes a candidate during a practical driving test on public roads in Cyprus, via Pexels.com*

Failing is more common than most people expect. The good news is that the same mistakes come up again and again. Fix these and you cut your risk significantly.

**Not checking mirrors enough.** This is the single biggest reason candidates fail. You must check your mirrors before every speed change, every signal, every manoeuvre, and every lane change. The examiner watches your eyes. If they cannot see you glancing at the mirror, they mark it.

**Poor observation at junctions.** Rolling slowly into a junction without looking both ways properly is a serious fault. You must make a real, visible check left and right before you move. On left-hand traffic roads, the approach direction changes, so make sure your habit is correct for Cyprus.

**Wrong lane use.** Choosing the wrong lane on a multi-lane road, or drifting across lane markings, picks up faults quickly. Plan your lane well before you need it.

**Failure to give way.** This includes not giving way to pedestrians at crossings and not yielding to traffic on a roundabout when required.

**Signalling too late or not at all.** Your signal should go on before you move, not while you are already moving.

**Stalling repeatedly.** One stall with a calm recovery is usually a minor fault. Stalling multiple times at the same junction suggests poor control and will cost you more marks.

## How Does the Mirror-Signal-Manoeuvre Routine Work in Cyprus?

The mirror-signal-manoeuvre (MSM) routine is not just a phrase your instructor says to fill lesson time. It is the core habit the examiner is looking for on every single move you make. Done consistently, it turns a hesitant test drive into a confident one.

Here is how the routine breaks down:

1. **Mirrors** - Check your interior mirror first, then the relevant door mirror. Do this before you do anything else.
2. **Signal** - Give a clear, timely signal in the direction you intend to go. Early enough for other road users to react.
3. **Manoeuvre** - Only then do you carry out the turn, lane change, or stop. Look again as you move.

In Cyprus, because traffic moves on the left, this routine works in mirror image compared to most of Europe. When turning right, you cross oncoming traffic. That makes the pre-turn mirror and observation checks even more important.

Your instructor will drill this into you. But many candidates do it correctly during lessons and then rush through it during the test because they are nervous. Slow down. The examiner is not marking you on speed. They are marking you on safety.

Instructors across Cyprus consistently name the MSM routine, along with correct roundabout use and accurate turn procedures, as the areas where examiners focus hardest. If you can make MSM a reflex rather than a thought, you remove one of the biggest failure risks from the test.

## What to Expect During the Driving Test?

Knowing what happens on the day reduces nerves. Here is the shape of a typical test in Cyprus.

You arrive at the test centre with your learner permit and any other documents required. Make sure your permit is valid. The learner permit is valid for two years and must not have expired. You must also have completed the required minimum number of lessons at a licensed driving school before you sit the test.

The examiner will introduce themselves and explain how the test works. They will give you instructions as you drive. Directions come in advance, so you have time to react. If you do not hear an instruction, it is fine to ask them to repeat it. That is not a fault.

The test takes place on public roads around the town or city of your test centre. Routes are set by the local centre and typically include a mix of:

- Urban streets with junctions and traffic lights
- Roundabouts
- Dual carriageways or rural roads, depending on your location

The drive lasts roughly 30 to 40 minutes. At the end, the examiner will tell you whether you have passed or failed. If you fail, they will explain the faults on your marking sheet. Listen carefully. That feedback is useful if you need to rebook.

## How to Handle Roundabouts and Difficult Junctions?

![Learner driver approaching a roundabout in left-hand traffic, signalling correctly from the correct lane](/blog/pass-driving-test-cyprus-first-time/inline-2.jpg)
*A learner driver practises the approach to a roundabout in left-hand traffic conditions, via Pexels.com*

Roundabouts catch more candidates off guard than almost anything else on the Cyprus test. The left-hand traffic system means the direction of flow is the opposite of what drivers from most EU countries are used to. Traffic on the roundabout comes from the right. You give way to it before entering.

Here is a simple approach to roundabouts that works for Cyprus:

1. Check your mirrors as you approach.
2. Signal your intention - left if you are taking the first exit, right if you are going further around.
3. Choose the correct lane before you reach the roundabout.
4. Look right and give way to traffic already on the roundabout.
5. Move off only when it is safe.
6. Signal left before your exit and check mirrors before you peel off.

At busy junctions, the same principles apply. Come in slowly enough to make a proper observation. Do not creep into the junction while still looking. Stop, look, then go.

Left turns at junctions in Cyprus are easier than right turns because you stay on the near side of the road. Right turns cross oncoming traffic. Take your time. Make sure the road is clear before you commit.

## What Are the Best Preparation Tips Before Your Test?

Passing first time is not just about raw hours behind the wheel. It is about smart preparation in the weeks and days before your test.

**Book enough lessons.** More lessons cost more money, yes. But failing and rebooking also costs time and money. Investing properly at the start is cheaper overall. You can compare [driving lesson costs in Cyprus](/en/blog/driving-lesson-cost-cyprus) to budget realistically before you begin.

**Use a licensed, accredited school.** Driving schools in Cyprus are regulated by the Department of Road Transport (ΤΟΜ). Always verify a school is properly accredited before you pay for lessons. An unregistered school cannot legally prepare you for the test.

**Ask your instructor about the local test route.** Test routes in Cyprus are fixed by each local test centre. A good instructor who works regularly in your area will know the common roads, junctions, and roundabouts you are likely to encounter. Practice them repeatedly until they feel normal.

**Do a mock test.** Ask your instructor to do a full mock test in silence, giving instructions the way an examiner would. This builds test-day composure.

**Sleep the night before.** It sounds basic. It matters. Tiredness slows your reaction time and makes you second-guess decisions you would normally make automatically.

**Arrive early.** Give yourself time to settle. Rushing to the test centre and arriving stressed does not help.

**Read the marking criteria.** Understanding what the examiner actually marks you on is not cheating. It is preparation. The Department of Road Transport is the official source for this information.

## What Happens if You Fail the Practical Driving Test?

Failing is disappointing. But it is also common, and it is not the end. Many drivers pass on their second or third attempt after using the feedback properly.

When the examiner gives you your result, they will go through the faults on the marking sheet. Pay close attention. They are telling you exactly what to work on. If you got a serious fault for poor junction observation, that is the thing you fix in your next set of lessons. If you accumulated minor faults for mirror checks, that is your focus.

After a fail, you must wait a minimum period before you can rebook the practical test. That period and the rebooking fee can change, so check current waiting times and fees directly with the Department of Road Transport (ΤΟΜ). Do not rely on second-hand information from friends, as rules and fees are updated periodically.

Use the waiting period well. Go back to your instructor. Tell them the faults from your sheet. A good instructor will redesign your remaining lessons around those specific weak points rather than repeating the same general practice you did before.

Your learner permit is valid for two years, so you have time to improve without starting the whole process again from scratch. If your permit is close to expiring, contact the Department of Road Transport about renewal options before it lapses.

Failing once does not predict failing again. Candidates who treat the fail as detailed feedback, rather than just bad luck, tend to pass the next time with a much cleaner sheet.

Passing the Cyprus driving test is genuinely achievable with the right preparation. The examiner is not looking for perfection. They are looking for consistent, safe habits. Mirror use, signalling, proper positioning, and calm observation at junctions are the foundation of every passing drive. Work with a licensed school, learn your local test route, and treat each lesson as practice for the real thing. Most people who fail once, pass the second time. Your goal is to make the first time count.

## FAQ

**Does Cyprus use the same marking system as the UK driving test?**
Cyprus drives on the left like the UK, and the testing structure shares similarities with the British system due to the country's colonial history. However, the test is run entirely by the Cyprus Department of Road Transport (ΤΟΜ) under its own regulations. You should not assume the rules or marking criteria are identical, so always get current information directly from ΤΟΜ.

**Can I take the Cyprus practical test in a car with an automatic gearbox?**
Yes, but if you pass in an automatic, your licence will restrict you to automatic vehicles only. If you want a full, unrestricted licence, you need to pass in a car with a manual gearbox. Discuss this with your driving school before you start lessons so you train in the right vehicle from the beginning.

**How long is the learner permit valid in Cyprus?**
The Cyprus learner permit (μαθητική άδεια) is valid for two years from the date of issue. It can be renewed if you have not yet passed your practical test by the time it expires. Contact the Department of Road Transport well before your permit expires to avoid any gap that could affect your test eligibility.

**Do I need to complete a minimum number of driving lessons before my practical test?**
Yes. Candidates in Cyprus must complete a required minimum number of lessons at a licensed driving school before they are eligible to sit the practical test. The exact number is set by regulation, so check the current requirements with the Department of Road Transport or your driving school when you enrol.

**What documents do I need to bring on the day of my driving test in Cyprus?**
At minimum, you will need your valid learner permit. Your driving school will advise you on any additional paperwork required for your specific test centre. Make sure all documents are in date and that you check the requirements with your school or the Department of Road Transport in advance, as missing a document can mean you cannot sit the test that day.

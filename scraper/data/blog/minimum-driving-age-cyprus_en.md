Cyprus applies EU Directive 2006/126/EC, which harmonises minimum ages across all member states.

So you want to get behind the wheel, or maybe on two wheels, and you need to know when the law actually lets you do that. The answer is not the same for every vehicle. Cyprus has a tiered system, and the age limits depend entirely on what you plan to drive. Understanding the rules early saves you from wasted trips to the Department of Road Transport.

## Why do different licence categories have different minimum ages?

The short answer is risk. Heavier and faster vehicles cause more damage in a crash. So the law makes you wait longer before you can drive them.

Cyprus follows EU Directive 2006/126/EC. This directive sets a common framework across all EU member states. It groups vehicles into categories, from tiny mopeds all the way to full-size buses. Each group has its own minimum age. Cyprus applies these rules directly. That means the age limits here are not a local invention. They are part of a system used across Europe.

The logic is straightforward. A 16-year-old on a 50cc moped is a manageable risk. A 16-year-old at the wheel of a 40-tonne lorry is not. So the minimum ages go up as the vehicle size and power go up.

There is also a progressive access idea built into the system. Some categories require you to hold a lower licence first, then wait, then upgrade. This is most visible with motorcycle categories, where you move up in stages. It is not just about age. It is about building experience over time.

For parents, this system means your child will not suddenly go from nothing to driving a powerful car. There are steps, checks, and waiting periods along the way. You can read more about how all the categories fit together in the guide to [Cyprus driving licence categories](/en/blog/cyprus-driving-licence-categories).

## Licences at 16: categories AM, A1, and B1

![Young person taking a driving lesson in Cyprus, AM licence moped category](/blog/minimum-driving-age-cyprus/inline-1.jpg)
*A young learner prepares for their first riding lesson in Cyprus, via Pexels.com*

Turning 16 in Cyprus opens up three licence categories. These are AM, A1, and B1. All three have a minimum age of 16.

**Category AM** covers mopeds and light vehicles with an engine up to 50cc and a maximum speed of 45 km/h. This is what most teenagers in Cyprus use to get to school or work without relying on parents. It is practical and relatively low-risk.

**Category A1** allows you to ride light motorcycles up to 125cc, with a maximum power of 11 kW. This is a real motorcycle, but with strict limits on engine size and power. It gives young riders more freedom than AM, while keeping the risk within a manageable range.

**Category B1** covers light quadricycles. Think four-wheeled micro vehicles, not standard cars. They are enclosed, they have four wheels, but they are lighter and slower than a Category B car. The minimum age is also 16.

For all three categories, you cannot simply show up and take a test. You must register with a licensed driving school in Cyprus. You will need to complete a set number of theory lessons and practical lessons before you sit the official tests. The exact number of required lessons is set by the Department of Road Transport, so check current requirements directly with them.

## Category B at 18: the standard car licence

Category B is what most people mean when they say "driving licence." It covers standard cars and light vans up to 3,500 kg. In Cyprus, you must be at least 18 years old to sit the Category B test.

This is the most common licence on the island. It covers the vehicles most families own and most jobs require. Once you hold it, you can drive any car within the weight limit without a supervisor.

You cannot just wait until you are 18 and then walk into a test centre. The process starts at a driving school. You enrol, study for the theory test, sit it, and then move on to practical lessons. The practical test comes after you complete the required hours. The whole process takes time, so starting your preparation a few months before your 18th birthday makes sense.

One thing parents often ask is whether their child can do any supervised driving before turning 18. Cyprus does not have a formal accompanied driving scheme like some other EU countries. Formal lessons with a licensed instructor are the required route here. Check with the Department of Road Transport for the latest rules on this.

## Motorcycles A2 and A: ages and progressive access explained

![Teenager on a motorcycle during a licence training session, A1 A2 learner rider](/blog/minimum-driving-age-cyprus/inline-2.jpg)
*A young rider completes practical training for a motorcycle licence in Cyprus, via Pexels.com*

Motorcycle licences in Cyprus follow a clear progression. The system is designed so that riders build experience before moving to more powerful bikes.

**Category A2** allows you to ride medium-power motorcycles. The minimum age is 18. You can sit for A2 directly at 18 without needing to hold A1 first. However, if you already hold A1 and have had it for at least two years, you can upgrade to A2 more easily.

**Category A** is the full, unrestricted motorcycle licence. There are two routes:

- **Progressive access:** If you hold Category A2 and have had it for at least two years, you can take the Category A test from age 20.
- **Direct access:** If you have not gone through A2, you must wait until you are 24 years old.

This two-year gap between A2 and full A is not just paperwork. It reflects real riding time. Riders who go through A2 first have two years of road experience before they get on an unrestricted bike. That experience matters.

For teenagers who are 16 and eager to ride, the path is: start with AM or A1 at 16, move to A2 at 18, then reach full A at 20 through progressive access. It is a four-year journey from first licence to no restrictions. That might feel long, but it is structured for a reason.

## Heavy vehicles and buses: categories C and D

Most teenagers will not need these categories right away. But some school leavers go straight into jobs that require them, so it is worth knowing the rules.

**Category C1** covers medium lorries, typically between 3,500 kg and 7,500 kg. The minimum age is 18.

**Category C** covers full heavy lorries over 7,500 kg. The minimum age is 21.

**Category D1** covers minibuses with more than eight passenger seats but no more than 16. The minimum age is 21.

**Category D** covers full-size buses with more than eight seats. The minimum age is also 21.

To hold any C or D category, you generally need to already hold Category B. You build up from the standard car licence and then apply for the heavier category. Each step requires its own theory test and practical test.

For anyone thinking about a career in transport or logistics, these categories are the goal. The road to them starts with Category B at 18, then C1 also at 18, then C or D at 21. There is no shortcut around the age limits. The EU directive that Cyprus follows does not allow exceptions on minimum age.

If you are a parent whose teenager wants to go into professional driving, encourage them to get their Category B sorted as early as possible. It is the foundation for everything else.

## Combination categories (trailers): what are the rules?

Combination categories cover vehicles towing trailers. These are labelled with an E after the base category: BE, C1E, CE, D1E, and DE.

The minimum age for each combination category mirrors the base category. So:

- BE (car with trailer) requires the same minimum age as Category B, which is 18.
- C1E (medium lorry with trailer) mirrors C1, so 18.
- CE (heavy lorry with trailer) mirrors C, so 21.
- D1E (minibus with trailer) mirrors D1, so 21.
- DE (bus with trailer) mirrors D, so 21.

You must hold the base licence before you can apply for the E combination. You cannot get CE without first holding C. The trailer licence is an addition, not a replacement.

In practice, most private drivers only ever need BE, if they want to tow a caravan or a boat trailer. This is more relevant to adults than teenagers. But for young people going into haulage or logistics, understanding CE from early on helps them plan their training path properly.

The combination licence requires its own practical test. It is not automatic once you hold the base category. Again, check with the Department of Road Transport for current fee structures, as these can change.

## How to start the process depending on your age

The steps differ depending on how old you are right now. Here is a practical breakdown:

**If you are 16:**
1. You can apply for AM, A1, or B1.
2. Find a licensed driving school and enrol.
3. Complete the required theory lessons and sit the theory test.
4. Complete practical lessons and sit the practical test.

**If you are 17:**
1. You are still too young for Category B.
2. If you do not already have AM or A1, this is a good time to get one.
3. You can prepare for Category B in advance so you are ready when you turn 18.

**If you are 18 or older:**
1. You can apply for Category B, A2, C1, or all of the above.
2. Enrol at a driving school, complete theory and practical requirements, then sit both tests.

Regardless of age, you must go through a licensed driving school. There is no self-study route that bypasses formal lessons. The school registers you with the Department of Road Transport and manages your application.

Costs vary between schools. Looking into [driving lesson costs in Cyprus](/en/blog/driving-lesson-cost-cyprus) before you commit helps you budget properly. Always verify exact fees with the Department of Road Transport, as they set the official test fee rates.

Getting your licence at the earliest legal age is not just about freedom. It opens up employment options and makes daily life easier on an island where public transport is limited outside the main cities.

The age limits in Cyprus are clear and consistent with EU law. ## FAQ

**What is the minimum age to ride a moped legally in Cyprus?**
The minimum age for a Category AM licence in Cyprus is 16 years old. This covers mopeds and light vehicles up to 50cc with a maximum speed of 45 km/h. You must complete lessons at a licensed driving school before sitting the tests.

**Can a 17-year-old start learning to drive a car in Cyprus?**
Cyprus does not currently have a formal accompanied driving programme that allows 17-year-olds to drive on public roads. Formal lessons with a licensed instructor at a registered driving school are the required route, and the minimum test age for Category B is 18.

**How does progressive access work for motorcycle licences in Cyprus?**
Progressive access means you move through licence categories in order. You can get A2 at 18, then after holding it for at least two years, you can take the full Category A test from age 20. Without going through A2 first, you must wait until you are 24 for direct access to Category A.

**Do trailer combination licences have their own separate age requirements?**
No. The minimum age for each combination category (BE, C1E, CE, D1E, DE) mirrors the minimum age of its base category. For example, CE requires the same minimum age as Category C, which is 21. You must hold the base licence before applying for the combination.

**Where do I find out the exact fees and lesson requirements for my licence category?**
Exact fees and minimum lesson requirements are set by the Department of Road Transport (ΤΟΜ) and can change. Always check current figures directly with ΤΟΜ. You can also speak to a licensed driving school, as they deal with the department regularly and will have up-to-date information.

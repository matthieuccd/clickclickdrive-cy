Cyprus is one of the few EU countries where driving feels oddly familiar to British visitors. The island drives on the left, a habit left over from British colonial rule. But familiar roads can hide unfamiliar rules, especially around licences. Whether you are here for two weeks or moving for good, knowing where you stand saves you real trouble.

## Is Your EU Licence Valid in Cyprus?

Yes, for most people. Cyprus and every other EU and EEA member state take part in mutual recognition of driving licences. That means if your licence was issued by an EU or EEA country, it is legally valid in Cyprus. You do not need to swap it, translate it, or get any extra paperwork just to drive here.

This works both ways. Your Cypriot licence, if you have one, is valid across the EU too. The system is built on trust between member states. Each country accepts that the other has tested its drivers properly.

There is one condition that catches people out. Your licence must still be valid. Driving with an expired foreign licence in Cyprus is not allowed, no matter where it was issued or how long you have lived here. Check the expiry date on your card before you get behind the wheel.

The rules change once you stop being a visitor and become a resident. That shift is where a lot of expats get confused. For now, if you are an EU or EEA citizen just living your life here without registering as a resident, your home-country licence keeps working. The detailed rules for [exchanging an EU driving licence in Cyprus](/en/blog/exchange-eu-licence-cyprus) are worth reading before your situation changes.

One more thing: this mutual recognition only covers EU and EEA licences. If your licence comes from outside that group, different rules apply entirely. Those are covered later in this article.

## How Long Can You Drive as a Visitor?

The answer depends on where your licence comes from.

If you hold an EU or EEA licence, there is no hard time limit while you remain a short-term visitor or non-resident. You can drive for the length of your stay, whether that is two weeks or several months. The key is that you have not established normal residence in Cyprus.

For visitors from outside the EU and EEA, the situation is more restricted. If you hold a valid International Driving Permit alongside your national licence, you may drive in Cyprus for up to 12 months from your date of entry. The IDP and the national licence must be carried together. One without the other is not enough.

Tourists from non-EU countries who do not have an IDP may still be able to use their national licence for a limited period. The general guideline has been up to six months, but this can vary. Rules in this area do change, so confirm the exact limit with ΤΟΜ before you rely on it.

A few practical points for visitors:

- Always carry your licence with you when driving.
- If you hold an IDP, carry your national licence alongside it at all times.
- Do not let your licence expire while you are here. An expired licence gives you no legal right to drive.
- If your visit stretches into long-term stay, the rules shift. Read on.

Cyprus roads can be busy around Limassol and Nicosia, and traffic police do carry out checks. Being caught without the right documents is not worth the risk.

## What Changes When You Become a Cyprus Resident?

This is the big one. The moment you establish normal residence in Cyprus, the rules change. You are no longer treated as a visitor. Cyprus law requires you to exchange your foreign licence for a Cypriot one.

"Normal residence" generally means that Cyprus is where you live most of the time, or where you have registered as a resident. For EU citizens, this typically happens when you register with the Civil Registry and Migration Department. For non-EU nationals, it usually follows from getting your residency permit.

Once that happens, you must apply to the Department of Road Transport (ΤΟΜ) to exchange your licence. For EU and EEA licence holders, the exchange process does not require you to sit a new driving test. Your existing licence is accepted as proof that you can drive. You hand it in, pay the applicable fees, and ΤΟΜ issues a Cypriot licence in its place.

Your original EU licence is not destroyed. ΤΟΜ keeps it on file. This is standard practice across the EU for cross-border exchanges.

The important word here is "required." This is not optional. Continuing to drive on your EU licence after becoming a Cyprus resident is not technically in line with the rules, even though the licence looks valid on its face. Do not leave the exchange too long after registering as a resident.

Check current fees with the Department of Road Transport. Costs and processing times do change, and the official ΤΟΜ website or their offices will have the most up-to-date figures.

## UK Licences After Brexit: What Rules Apply?

Before 2021, UK licences were treated exactly like any other EU licence in Cyprus. Brexit changed that. The UK is no longer an EU or EEA member, so UK licences no longer benefit from mutual recognition.

In practice, this means UK nationals living in Cyprus now fall under the same general framework as other third-country nationals. If you are a UK citizen who has established normal residence in Cyprus, you cannot simply exchange your UK licence the same way an EU citizen would. You will need to go through ΤΟΜ and follow the process that applies to non-EU licence holders.

The full picture is more detailed than a single paragraph can cover. UK visitors to Cyprus who are not residents are generally still able to drive using their UK licence for short stays, but again, verify the current rules with ΤΟΜ. Policies in this area have been subject to change since Brexit.

## Third-Country Licences and the International Driving Permit

If your licence was issued by a country outside the EU and EEA, and you are not a UK national, you fall into the third-country category. The rules here are different and worth understanding clearly.

The most widely recognised tool for driving abroad is the International Driving Permit. An IDP is a document issued in your home country that translates your licence details into multiple languages. It is not a licence on its own. It works alongside your national licence.

In Cyprus, an IDP holder may drive for up to 12 months from the date of entry. After that point, you will need to obtain a Cypriot licence if you are resident here.

Some countries have a bilateral agreement with Cyprus. These agreements can simplify the exchange process, sometimes allowing a more direct swap rather than requiring you to go through full licence acquisition. The list of eligible countries is managed by ΤΟΜ and does change over time. Always check directly with ΤΟΜ to see whether your country is on the current list.

If your country does not have a bilateral agreement with Cyprus and your licence does not qualify for direct exchange, you may need to enrol in a Cypriot driving school and go through the full process. This is covered in the next section.

A few things to note:

- Your IDP must accompany your national licence at all times while driving.
- An IDP issued in Cyprus is for use abroad, not for driving within Cyprus itself.
- If your national licence has expired, your IDP is also considered invalid.

## How Does the Licence Exchange Process Work?

The exchange process is handled by the Department of Road Transport. The broad steps are similar whether you hold an EU licence or a third-country licence that qualifies for exchange, though the exact requirements differ.

For EU and EEA licence holders, the process is relatively straightforward:

1. Confirm that you have established normal residence in Cyprus.
2. Gather your documents. You will typically need your valid EU licence, proof of residence, a passport or national ID, and passport-sized photos.
3. Submit your application to ΤΟΜ.
4. Pay the applicable fee. Check current fees with the Department of Road Transport.
5. Your original EU licence is surrendered and held by ΤΟΜ.
6. Your Cypriot licence is issued.

No driving test is required for EU and EEA holders going through a standard exchange.

For third-country nationals using a bilateral agreement route, the steps are broadly similar, but the documentation requirements may differ. ΤΟΜ will tell you exactly what is needed for your country of origin.

Processing times vary. It is worth starting the process early rather than waiting until your old licence is close to expiry. ΤΟΜ offices can be busy, especially in the main cities.

## When Do You Need a Cyprus Driving School?

Not everyone can exchange their licence directly. If your licence does not qualify for a straight swap, because it comes from a country with no bilateral agreement or because it falls outside the exchange rules, you will need to obtain a Cypriot licence from scratch.

That means enrolling in an approved Cyprus driving school. You will need to complete the required theory training and practical driving lessons. You will also need to pass both a theory test and a practical driving test.

This is the same path a young Cypriot driver takes when getting their first licence. For experienced drivers, it can feel unnecessary. But the requirement exists, and skipping it is not an option.

Cyprus joined the EU's graduated licensing framework, and the standards here meet EU requirements. The theory test covers road signs, rules, and hazard awareness. The practical test takes place on public roads with an examiner.

If you are heading down this route, it helps to know what to expect. The [Cyprus driving test guide](/en/blog/cyprus-driving-test-2026) covers the current test format, what examiners look for, and how to prepare.

Driving schools in Cyprus vary in quality and price. Ask around locally for recommendations, especially among other expats who have been through the process. Some schools have English-speaking instructors, which matters if your Greek is limited.

One thing worth knowing: Cyprus drives on the left. If you learned to drive in a left-hand-traffic country, you will adapt quickly. If you are used to driving on the right, you will need some time to adjust, and a few lessons with a local instructor before your test will help.

Getting your Cypriot licence properly set up is one of those practical steps that makes daily life here much easier. Once it is done, you have a full EU licence that works across all 27 member states.

The rules around foreign licences in Cyprus are not always simple, but they are manageable once you know which category you fall into. EU and EEA holders have the easiest path. UK nationals need to check their position carefully after Brexit. Third-country nationals should look into the IDP route for short stays and the exchange or school route for longer ones. In every case, the Department of Road Transport is the right place to get current, official guidance tailored to your situation.

## FAQ

**Can I drive in Cyprus with my EU licence without exchanging it?**
Yes, as long as you have not established normal residence in Cyprus. EU and EEA licences are mutually recognised, so visitors and short-term non-residents can drive freely. Once you become a resident, you are required to exchange your licence for a Cypriot one.

**Does exchanging my EU licence for a Cypriot one mean I have to take a driving test?**
No. EU and EEA licence holders who go through the standard exchange process do not need to pass a new driving test. Your existing licence is accepted as valid proof of your driving qualification, and ΤΟΜ issues a Cypriot licence in return.

**What happens to my original EU licence when I exchange it?**
ΤΟΜ takes your original licence and keeps it on file. This is standard practice for licence exchanges within the EU framework. You will not get it back, but your Cypriot licence replaces it and is valid throughout the EU.

**Can I use my non-EU licence to drive in Cyprus as a tourist?**
Yes, for a limited period. If you hold a valid International Driving Permit alongside your national licence, you may drive for up to 12 months from your date of entry. Without an IDP, a shorter period may apply. Confirm the exact rules with ΤΟΜ before you travel, as requirements can change.

**Do UK licence holders need to take a full driving test to get a Cypriot licence?**
This depends on your individual circumstances and any agreements currently in place. Since Brexit, UK licences are no longer treated as EU licences in Cyprus, which means UK nationals cannot use the standard EU exchange route.

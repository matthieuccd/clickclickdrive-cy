To get a Cyprus driving licence, you need to complete four steps: apply for a learner's licence at the Department of Road Transport, complete lessons with a licensed instructor, pass the theory test, and pass the practical test. The process typically takes three to six months for a new driver. EU citizens can exchange their existing licence without sitting tests; other nationalities should check their status with the Department.

Getting a driving licence in Cyprus is very doable. But the process has more steps than many people expect, and the rules differ depending on where you are from. Whether you have never driven before or you are moving here with a licence from another country, this guide covers everything you need to know for 2026.

## How the Cyprus driving licence system works

Cyprus has one single authority for driving licences. That is the Department of Road Transport, known in Greek as the Τμήμα Οδικών Μεταφορών. It sits under the Ministry of Transport, Communications and Works. No private company, municipality, or other body can issue a Cypriot licence. Only the Department can do that.

The Department has its main office in Nicosia. There are also offices in Limassol, Larnaca, Paphos, and Paralimni. If you live outside Nicosia, you can deal with your nearest regional office for most steps. This saves a lot of travelling.

Cyprus driving licences follow EU standards. The categories, the format, and the rules are aligned with the rest of the European Union. A licence issued here is valid across all EU member states. That matters a lot if you plan to drive when you travel to Europe.

One thing that catches newcomers off guard: Cyprus drives on the left. If you come from a country that drives on the right, this takes real adjustment. The road signs use kilometres and km/h, not miles. Speed limits, distances, and all road markings follow the metric system.

The Republic of Cyprus covers the southern part of the island. The north operates under a separate system and a different authority. A Cypriot licence issued by the Department of Road Transport does not automatically apply in the north. This guide covers the Republic of Cyprus only.

## Who can apply and the minimum age

To apply for a standard Category B licence in Cyprus, you must be at least 18 years old. Category B covers everyday passenger cars up to 3,500 kg and carrying no more than eight passengers. This is the licence most people in Cyprus are after.

You also need to be resident in the Republic of Cyprus. The Department asks for proof of this. Accepted documents include a rental contract, a recent utility bill, or your yellow slip (also called the ARC - Alien Registration Certificate). If you are an EU citizen registered as a resident, your registration certificate works too.

Beyond age and residency, you need a valid passport or national ID card. You will also need a recent passport-style photograph and a medical certificate. The medical certificate must come from a registered doctor in Cyprus. It confirms you are fit to drive. Your own GP can usually provide this. The doctor checks your eyesight and general health. If you wear glasses or contact lenses, that gets noted on the licence.

There is no upper age limit to apply for a first licence. However, once you pass age 70, renewal requires additional medical checks. More on that in the section on validity below.

If you are the parent or guardian of a teenager approaching 18, it is worth knowing there is no provisional licence system for younger learners in Cyprus. The process starts at 18, full stop.

## The learner's licence: your first official step

Before you sit behind the wheel of a car for a lesson, you need a learner's licence. In Greek this is called the εκπαιδευτική άδεια. You apply for it at the Department of Road Transport. You cannot legally start driving lessons without it.

Here is what you bring to the Department when you apply:

- Valid passport or national ID
- Proof of residence in Cyprus (rental contract, utility bill, or yellow slip / ARC)
- A recent passport-style photograph
- A medical certificate from a registered doctor confirming fitness to drive

Check current fees with the Department of Road Transport. Fees are set by the Department and can change. Do not rely on figures you find on forums or older websites.

Once your application is approved, you receive your learner's licence. From that point, you can start booking lessons with a licensed driving instructor. When you are at the wheel during a lesson, the car must display red L plates on the front and rear at all times. This is a legal requirement, not just a courtesy to other drivers.

The learner's licence is tied to the lessons process. It is not a full licence and does not allow you to drive independently. You must always be supervised by a licensed instructor in a dual-control car.

Keep a copy of everything you submit. The Department is generally well organised, but having your own records avoids headaches if anything needs to be checked later.

## Driving lessons: what to expect

Driving lessons in Cyprus are done with a licensed instructor in a dual-control car. The dual controls mean the instructor can brake or intervene if needed. This is standard across all licensed schools.

Each lesson typically lasts 45 minutes. How many lessons you need depends on your experience and how quickly you pick things up. Some people need 20 lessons. Others need 40 or more. There is no fixed minimum number set by law for Category B, but your instructor will tell you honestly when they think you are ready to test.

Lesson prices vary between schools and between cities. Nicosia tends to be slightly different from smaller towns. Shopping around is completely normal and advisable. Ask for a price per lesson and ask whether the school charges separately for test-day accompaniment. Some do, some do not.

Lessons usually take place on real roads from early on. Cyprus does not have a dedicated learner track system. You will drive in traffic, on roundabouts, on dual carriageways, and through town centres. This is actually a good thing. It means that by the time you test, you have real experience.

## The theory test: what it covers and how to pass

The theory test in Cyprus is computer-based. You sit at a screen and answer multiple-choice questions. You can take the test in Greek or in English. For most expats and newcomers, English is the obvious choice.

The test covers three main areas:

- Road signs and their meanings
- Traffic rules and right-of-way situations
- Safe driving behaviour and hazard awareness

You book the theory test through the Department of Road Transport. Your driving school can usually help with this. Check current fees with the Department before booking.

Preparation matters. The questions are drawn from an official question bank. Many driving schools give you access to practice software that mirrors the real test format. Using this regularly in the weeks before your test makes a big difference. Do not assume that knowing how to drive means you know the theory. The sign recognition questions in particular catch people out.

Cypriot road signs follow EU standards, so if you already know European signs, you have a head start. But the specific traffic rules and local priority rules are worth studying carefully.

There is a pass mark required. If you do not pass, you can rebook and try again. There is no long waiting period between attempts, but you will pay the test fee each time. Passing the theory test is a condition for booking the practical test. You cannot sit the practical until the theory is done.

## The practical test: what happens on the day

Once you have passed the theory test, you can book the practical test. Your instructor will usually advise you when you are ready. Do not rush this stage. Failing the practical and rebooking costs time and money.

The practical test is conducted on real public roads by an examiner from the Department of Road Transport. It is not done in a closed car park or on a private circuit. You will drive through actual traffic, so the conditions on the day affect the experience. Morning slots in busy city centres are harder than quieter afternoon slots in smaller towns.

On the day, your instructor will typically accompany you to the test centre. The examiner sits in the passenger seat. Your instructor usually waits at the test centre. The test lasts around 30 to 45 minutes depending on the route.

The examiner watches for:

- Correct use of mirrors and signals
- Smooth control of the car
- Correct approach to junctions, roundabouts, and pedestrian crossings
- Speed management in different zones
- Parking and reversing manoeuvres

Remember: Cyprus drives on the left. If you trained in a right-hand-drive country, this is ingrained. If you came from a left-hand-drive country, make sure your instructor has spent real time drilling left-side driving habits with you before the test.

If you pass, the examiner confirms this on the day. Your full licence is then issued by the Department. If you do not pass, you receive feedback and can rebook. There is no fixed limit on attempts, but each attempt requires a new fee.

## Licence categories, validity, and renewal

Cyprus follows the standard EU licence category system. The categories most people encounter are:

- **AM** - mopeds and light quadricycles
- **A1** - light motorcycles (restricted power)
- **A2** - medium motorcycles (restricted power)
- **A** - full motorcycle licence (unrestricted)
- **B** - passenger cars up to 3,500 kg, up to 8 passengers
- **C** - trucks and heavy goods vehicles
- **D** - buses and passenger-carrying vehicles
- **BE / CE / DE** - the above categories with trailer combinations

For most people reading this, Category B is the goal. It covers the standard family car and is what the bulk of this guide is about.

A Cyprus Category B licence is valid across all EU member states. This is one of the practical benefits of holding a Cypriot licence rather than keeping a non-EU licence.

In terms of validity, a Category B licence is typically valid until the holder reaches age 70. After that, renewal requires medical checks to confirm continued fitness to drive. Check current validity rules directly with the Department of Road Transport, as these rules can be updated.

If you hold a Cyprus licence and move to another EU country, the exchange process works in reverse - the other country's rules apply. If you stay in Cyprus, your licence remains valid until the renewal date printed on the card.

Professional categories like C and D have shorter validity periods and require periodic medical checks throughout. If you drive for work, check the specific rules for your category with the Department.

## If you already hold a foreign driving licence

This is where things get more varied. Your rights in Cyprus depend heavily on which country issued your licence.

**EU and EEA licence holders** living in Cyprus may continue driving on their home licence indefinitely. However, there is a window after taking up official residence during which you can exchange your EU licence for a Cypriot one without sitting any new tests. No theory test, no practical test. Just paperwork and the relevant fee. The exact window period can change, so check current rules with the Department. For full details on this process, read the guide on [exchanging an EU licence](/en/blog/exchange-eu-licence-cyprus).

**UK licence holders** are in a different position after Brexit. The UK is now treated as a third country for exchange purposes. This means a certified translation of your licence is likely required. Depending on the current bilateral agreement between Cyprus and the UK, you may also need to sit one or both tests. The situation has evolved since Brexit and it is worth checking the current position directly with the Department before you do anything. The article on [UK licence in Cyprus after Brexit](/en/blog/uk-licence-cyprus-after-brexit) covers this in detail.

**Third-country nationals** with licences from countries not on the Department's recognised list typically go through the full process: learner's licence, lessons, theory test, and practical test. This is the same path as a brand new driver. Some countries have bilateral agreements with Cyprus that allow partial or full exchange. The only way to know for sure is to ask the Department directly about your specific country.

Whatever your background, bring your original licence, a certified translation if your licence is not in Greek or English, your proof of residency, and your passport when you visit the Department to ask about your options.

Getting a driving licence in Cyprus takes patience, but the system is clear once you understand how it works. The Department of Road Transport staff are used to dealing with people from many different countries. If you go prepared with the right documents and the right questions, the process moves much more smoothly. Start with the step that applies to you, take it one stage at a time, and you will have your Cypriot licence sooner than you think.

## FAQ

**How long does it take to get a driving licence in Cyprus from scratch?**
Most new drivers complete the process in three to six months. This depends on how quickly you book lessons, how soon you feel ready for the tests, and how busy the Department's test schedule is at the time you apply.

**Can I drive in Cyprus while waiting for my licence to be processed?**
If you hold a valid EU licence, you can drive on it while living in Cyprus. If you are a new learner awaiting your learner's licence, you cannot drive at all until the learner's licence is issued and you are supervised by a licensed instructor.

**Do I need to speak Greek to pass the theory test?**
No. The theory test is available in English as well as Greek. You choose your preferred language when you book. Most expats and newcomers take it in English without any issues.

**Is a Cyprus driving licence valid in the UK after Brexit?**
A Cyprus driving licence is an EU licence and is generally recognised for driving in the UK for visits. However, if you become a UK resident, different rules apply. Always check the current position with the UK DVLA and the Department of Road Transport before relying on your Cypriot licence abroad.

**What happens if I move to Northern Cyprus - does my Republic of Cyprus licence work there?**
The Republic of Cyprus and Northern Cyprus operate separate systems. A licence issued by the Department of Road Transport in the south does not automatically apply in the north. The two areas are governed by different authorities, and the practical and legal situation in the north is complex. Take local advice before driving there.

The Cyprus provisional driving licence, known as the ekpedeftiki adeia, is issued by the Department of Road Transport (TOM) and must be obtained before you can take any driving lessons. You can apply from the age of 17 by submitting an identity document, a medical certificate, an eye test result, and passport photos in person at your local TOM district office. Once issued, the licence allows you to drive only in a dual-control vehicle with a registered instructor.

Getting a driving licence in Cyprus takes time and planning. The process has several stages, and the provisional licence is the very first one. Many people, especially expats and parents of teenagers, find the system confusing at first glance. This guide walks you through every step in plain English so you know exactly what to expect.

## What is a provisional driving licence in Cyprus?

The provisional driving licence in Cyprus is officially called the εκπαιδευτική άδεια οδήγησης, which translates roughly as "training driving licence." Most people just call it the ekpedeftiki adeia. It is issued by the Department of Road Transport, known in Greek as the Τμήμα Οδικών Μεταφορών, or ΤΟΜ for short.

Think of it as a learner permit. It does not let you drive on your own. It exists so that you can sit behind the wheel legally while a qualified instructor teaches you. Without it, no registered driving school in Cyprus can take you out on the road.

The licence is tied to a specific vehicle category. For most new drivers, that means Category B, which covers standard passenger cars. If you want to understand the full range of categories available in Cyprus, the [Cyprus driving licence categories](/en/blog/cyprus-driving-licence-categories) guide explains each one clearly.

The ekpedeftiki adeia is not a full licence and carries legal restrictions. You can only drive in a dual-control vehicle. The car must be registered with a licensed driving school. And a qualified instructor must be in the vehicle with you at all times. There is no legal grey area here. Driving alone on a provisional licence will get you in serious trouble.

## What are the eligibility requirements to apply?

The rules around eligibility are straightforward. Cyprus keeps them simple so that young people and newcomers can start the process without too much difficulty.

The minimum age to apply for a Category B provisional driving licence is 17. You do not need to wait until you are 18. This means a parent or guardian can start the process on behalf of a teenager, or a 17-year-old can go in themselves with the right documents.

There is no upper age limit. Adults of any age who are new to Cyprus or who have never held a licence can apply. Expats who arrived in Cyprus without a recognised driving licence go through exactly the same process as a 17-year-old local resident.

You must be medically fit to drive. This is not just a formality. Cyprus requires a real medical certificate from a doctor confirming that you have no conditions that would prevent safe driving. You also need to pass an eye examination. The eye test checks that your vision meets the minimum standard required to hold a Category B licence.

Beyond age and health, you need a valid identity document. If you are an EU citizen, your national ID card or passport works fine. Non-EU residents should bring their passport. Residency documents may also be requested, so it is worth calling your local TOM office before your visit to confirm what they need in your specific situation.

You do not need to book a theory test or demonstrate any driving knowledge at this stage. The provisional licence application is purely about confirming your identity, your age, and your basic medical fitness.

## Which documents do you need for the application?

Getting your paperwork right before you go to the TOM office saves you a wasted trip. Here is what you need to bring:

- A valid national ID card or passport
- A medical certificate confirming fitness to drive, issued by a registered doctor
- A completed eye examination result (οφθαλμολογική εξέταση), from a qualified ophthalmologist or optician
- Two recent passport-sized photographs
- The completed application form (you can get this at the TOM office or ask your driving school for a copy in advance)

A few practical notes on these documents. For the medical certificate, visit your GP or a private doctor. Explain that you need a certificate for a driving licence application. Most doctors in Cyprus are familiar with this request. For the eye test, an ophthalmologist is the safest choice, though some TOM offices also accept results from registered opticians. Ask your driving school which they recommend locally.

For the photos, bring two copies. They should be recent, plain background, and passport-standard. Any photo shop in Cyprus can produce these quickly and cheaply.

Your driving school can be a real help here. Many [driving schools in Nicosia](/en/driving-schools/nicosia) and across Cyprus regularly assist students with gathering the right documents and checking everything before submission. Do not be shy about asking them.

## Where and how do you submit the application step by step?

The application must be submitted in person. You cannot do this online. You cannot send someone else unless they have legal authority to act on your behalf. You must walk into a TOM district office yourself.

Here is the process from start to finish:

1. Book or prepare your medical certificate and eye test results first. These take a little time to arrange, so sort them before anything else.
2. Collect your passport photos - two copies - from any photo shop.
3. Download or collect the application form. Your driving school may have printed copies. Alternatively, pick one up at the TOM office on the day.
4. Go to your nearest TOM district office. There are offices in Nicosia, Limassol, Larnaca, Paphos, and Famagusta (for the government-controlled area).
5. Take a number and wait to be called. Bring all your documents in a folder so nothing gets missed.
6. Hand over your documents at the counter. The officer will check everything and process your application.
7. Pay the applicable fee at the office. Keep your receipt.
8. Wait for the provisional licence to be issued. In many cases this happens on the same day or within a short turnaround period. Ask the officer at the counter for the expected timeframe.

Going early in the morning tends to mean shorter queues. The [step-by-step driving licence guide](/en/blog/how-to-get-driving-licence-cyprus-step-by-step) has more detail on the wider process if you want to see how this visit fits into the full journey.

## How much does the provisional licence cost?

Fees for the provisional driving licence are set by the Department of Road Transport. They can change over time. For that reason, this article does not quote a specific figure. Always check current fees with the Department of Road Transport directly before your visit. You can call your nearest TOM district office or check the TOM website for the latest information.

What you can expect is that the fee is paid at the counter when you submit your application. Cash is usually accepted. It is worth asking in advance whether card payment is available at your specific office, as this varies.

## What happens after your provisional licence is issued?

Once you have the ekpedeftiki adeia in your hand, the next stage begins. You are now legally allowed to sit in the driver's seat of a dual-control car and learn. But there are clear rules about what comes next.

You must take both theory lessons and practical driving lessons at a licensed Cyprus driving school. This is not optional. TOM requires candidates to complete a set number of lessons before they can sit the theory test and the road test. The exact number is something your driving school will explain based on your category and progress.

Your driving school will guide you through the theory curriculum. In Cyprus, the theory test covers road signs, traffic rules, and safe driving behaviour. Most schools provide materials in English as well as Greek. Ask about this when you enrol if English is your main language.

The practical lessons build your skills behind the wheel. Your instructor will decide when you are ready to sit the road test. Do not rush this stage. Being well-prepared before you sit the test saves time and money in the long run.

For full details on what the tests involve and how to prepare, the [Cyprus driving test guide](/en/blog/cyprus-driving-test-2026) covers both the theory and practical elements in depth.

One thing worth knowing: the provisional licence has a validity period. If you take too long to progress through your lessons and tests, you may need to renew it. Check this with TOM or your driving school so you do not let it lapse unexpectedly.

The whole process - from provisional licence to full licence - takes most people several months. Some take longer. That is completely normal. The key is to stay consistent with your lessons and not leave long gaps between them.

Getting your provisional licence sorted early, before the summer rush or before the academic year starts, gives you more flexibility with lesson scheduling. Driving schools get busy. Book your place as soon as your ekpedeftiki adeia arrives.

## FAQ

**What is the ekpedeftiki adeia and why do I need it?**
The ekpedeftiki adeia is the official Greek name for the Cyprus provisional driving licence. You need it before you can legally take any driving lessons, as it is the document that permits you to drive in a dual-control vehicle under the supervision of a registered instructor.

**Can a 17-year-old apply for the provisional licence without a parent present?**
Yes, the minimum age to apply is 17, and there is no rule requiring a parent or guardian to be present at the TOM office. However, if the applicant is a minor, it is worth confirming current requirements with your local TOM district office before the visit, as practices can vary slightly.

**Do I need a Cyprus doctor for the medical certificate, or can I use a certificate from abroad?**
The medical certificate must confirm fitness to drive and is generally expected to come from a doctor practising in Cyprus. A certificate issued abroad is unlikely to be accepted. Visit a local GP or private clinic and explain that the certificate is for a driving licence application.

**How long does it take to get the provisional licence after I submit my application?**
In many cases the ekpedeftiki adeia is issued on the same day you submit your documents at the TOM office. However, this can vary by office and by how busy they are. Ask the officer at the counter for the expected turnaround when you submit.

**What happens if my provisional licence expires before I pass my tests?**
If your provisional licence lapses, you will need to renew it before you can continue lessons or sit your tests. Contact your local TOM district office for guidance on the renewal process and any fees that apply. Your driving school can also advise you on this.
